## 1. Chemical Reaction & Chemical Equation

### Chemical Reaction

A process in which one or more substances (reactants) undergo a chemical change to form new substances (products) with entirely different chemical properties.

**Common Indicators of a Chemical Reaction:**

- Change in state
    
- Change in color
    
- Evolution of a gas ($\uparrow$)
    
- Change in temperature (heat absorbed or released)
    
- Formation of a precipitate ($\downarrow$)
    

### Chemical Equation

A symbolic representation of a chemical reaction using element symbols and chemical formulas.

- **Reactants:** Written on the Left-Hand Side (LHS).
    
- **Products:** Written on the Right-Hand Side (RHS).
    
- **Physical States:** Specified using $(s)$ for solid, $(l)$ for liquid, $(g)$ for gas, and $(aq)$ for aqueous solution.
    

```
Reactants (LHS)  ───►  Products (RHS)
```

## 2. Balancing Chemical Equations

### Law of Conservation of Mass

> _Mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction._

Therefore, the **total number of atoms of each element on the reactant side must equal the number of atoms on the product side**.

### Step-by-Step Method (Hit-and-Trial)

To balance: $\text{Fe} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4 + \text{H}_2$

|**Element**|**Atoms in Reactants (LHS)**|**Atoms in Products (RHS)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Fe**|1|3|
|**H**|2|2|
|**O**|1|4|

1. **Balance Oxygen ($O$):** Multiply $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ by $4 \implies \text{Fe} + \mathbf{4}\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4 + \text{H}_2$
    
2. **Balance Hydrogen ($H$):** Multiply $\text{H}_2$ by $4 \implies \text{Fe} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4 + \mathbf{4}\text{H}_2$
    
3. **Balance Iron ($Fe$):** Multiply $\text{Fe}$ by $3 \implies \mathbf{3}\text{Fe} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4 + 4\text{H}_2$
    

**Final Balanced Equation:**

$$3\text{Fe}(s) + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}(g) \longrightarrow \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4(s) + 4\text{H}_2(g)$$

## 3. Types of Chemical Reactions

### 1. Combination Reaction

A reaction where **two or more reactants combine to form a single product**.

$$\text{A} + \text{B} \longrightarrow \text{AB}$$

- **Example (Slaking of Lime):**
    
    $$\text{CaO}(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \longrightarrow \text{Ca(OH)}_2(aq) + \text{Heat}$$
    
    - $\text{CaO}$: Quicklime
        
    - $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$: Slaked lime (used for whitewashing walls)
        

### 2. Decomposition Reaction

A reaction where **a single reactant breaks down into two or more simpler products**.

$$\text{AB} \longrightarrow \text{A} + \text{B}$$

Decomposition reactions require energy in the form of heat, light, or electricity:

|**Type**|**Energy Source**|**Key Example**|
|---|---|---|
|**Thermal Decomposition**|Heat|$2\text{FeSO}_4(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3(s) + \text{SO}_2(g) + \text{SO}_3(g)$<br><br>  <br><br>_(Green crystals turn reddish-brown)_|
|**Electrolytic Decomposition**|Electricity|$2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \xrightarrow{\text{Electricity}} 2\text{H}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$|
|**Photolytic Decomposition**|Sunlight|$2\text{AgCl}(s) \xrightarrow{\text{Sunlight}} 2\text{Ag}(s) + \text{Cl}_2(g)$<br><br>  <br><br>_(White silver chloride turns grey; used in black-and-white photography)_|

### 3. Displacement Reaction

A reaction in which **a more reactive element displaces a less reactive element** from its salt solution.

$$\text{A} + \text{BC} \longrightarrow \text{AC} + \text{B}$$

- **Example:**
    
    $$\text{Fe}(s) + \text{CuSO}_4(aq) \longrightarrow \text{FeSO}_4(aq) + \text{Cu}(s)$$
    
    - The blue color of Copper Sulphate ($\text{CuSO}_4$) fades to pale green ($\text{FeSO}_4$), and reddish-brown copper deposits on the iron nail.
        

### 4. Double Displacement Reaction

A reaction where **two compounds exchange their ions** to form two new compounds.

$$\text{AB} + \text{CD} \longrightarrow \text{AD} + \text{CB}$$

- **Example (Precipitation Reaction):**
    
    $$\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4(aq) + \text{BaCl}_2(aq) \longrightarrow \text{BaSO}_4(s)\downarrow + 2\text{NaCl}(aq)$$
    
    - Forms a white insoluble precipitate of Barium Sulphate ($\text{BaSO}_4$).
        

### 5. Oxidation and Reduction (Redox Reactions)

```
       Gain of Oxygen / Loss of Hydrogen = OXIDATION
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │                                                  ▼
CuO(s)  +  H₂(g)  ─── Heat ───►  Cu(s)  +  H₂O(l)
  │                                           ▲
  └───────────────────────────────────────────┘
       Loss of Oxygen / Gain of Hydrogen = REDUCTION
```

- **Oxidation:** Gain of oxygen OR loss of hydrogen.
    
- **Reduction:** Loss of oxygen OR gain of hydrogen.
    
- **Redox Reaction:** A reaction where oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously.
    
- **Oxidizing Agent:** The substance that gets reduced (provides oxygen or accepts hydrogen).
    
- **Reducing Agent:** The substance that gets oxidized (accepts oxygen or provides hydrogen).
    

## 4. Energy Changes in Reactions

- **Exothermic Reactions:** Chemical reactions that **release heat energy** along with product formation.
    
    - _Example:_ Natural gas burning: $\text{CH}_4(g) + 2\text{O}_2(g) \longrightarrow \text{CO}_2(g) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(g) + \text{Heat}$
        
    - _Note:_ **Respiration** is considered an exothermic reaction.
        
- **Endothermic Reactions:** Chemical reactions that **absorb heat/energy** to proceed.
    
    - _Example:_ $\text{CaCO}_3(s) + \text{Heat} \longrightarrow \text{CaO}(s) + \text{CO}_2(g)$
        

## 5. Effects of Oxidation in Daily Life

### 1. Corrosion

The gradual degradation of metals when exposed to moisture, air, acids, or chemicals over time.

- **Rusting of Iron:** $4\text{Fe} + 3\text{O}_2 + x\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow 2\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3\cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (Hydrated ferric oxide - reddish brown)
    
- **Black coating on Silver:** Silver reacts with $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ in air to form Silver Sulphide ($\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$).
    
- **Green coating on Copper:** Copper forms Basic Copper Carbonate ($\text{CuCO}_3\cdot\text{Cu(OH)}_2$).
    
- **Prevention:** Galvanization (zinc coating), painting, greasing, alloying, or electroplating.
    

### 2. Rancidity

The oxidation of fats and oils in food items when left exposed to air, leading to an unpleasant smell and taste.

- **Prevention:**
    
    - Flushing packaging bags with inert gas like **Nitrogen** ($\text{N}_2$).
        
    - Adding **antioxidants** to foods containing fats and oils.
        
    - Storing food in **airtight containers** or **refrigerators**.
