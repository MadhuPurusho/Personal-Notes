## 1. What is a Resource?

A **resource** is everything available in our environment that can be used to satisfy our needs, provided it is:

1. **Technologically accessible**
    
2. **Economically feasible**
    
3. **Culturally acceptable**
    

### Interdependence of Nature, Technology, and Institutions

Human beings interact with nature through **technology** and create **institutions** to accelerate their economic development.

1. **On the Basis of Origin:**
    
    - **Biotic:** Obtained from the biosphere and have life (e.g., flora, fauna, human beings, livestock).
        
    - **Abiotic:** Composed of non-living things (e.g., rocks, metals, soils).
        
2. **On the Basis of Exhaustibility:**
    
    - **Renewable:** Can be renewed or reproduced by physical, chemical, or mechanical processes (e.g., solar/wind energy, water, forests).
        
    - **Non-Renewable:** Occur over very long geological times; take millions of years to form (e.g., minerals, fossil fuels).
        
3. **On the Basis of Ownership:**
    
    - **Individual:** Owned privately by individuals (e.g., farmland, plots, houses).
        
    - **Community:** Accessible to all members of a community (e.g., village ponds, public parks, burial grounds).
        
    - **National:** Belonging to the nation (all resources within political boundaries and territorial sea up to **12 nautical miles** / ~22.2 km from the coast).
        
    - **International:** Regulated by international institutions (oceanic resources beyond **200 nautical miles** of the Exclusive Economic Zone).
        
4. **On the Basis of the Status of Development:**
    
    - **Potential Resources:** Found in a region but have not been utilized (e.g., wind energy potential in Rajasthan and Gujarat).
        
    - **Developed Resources:** Surveyed and their quality/quantity determined for utilization.
        
    - **Stock:** Materials in the environment with potential to satisfy human needs, but humans lack appropriate technology to access them (e.g., using water as a source of hydrogen fuel).
        
    - **Reserves:** Subset of stock that can be put into use with existing technical know-how, but conserved for future needs (e.g., water in dams, forests).
        

## 3. Development of Resources & Sustainable Development

### Problems Caused by Indiscriminate Resource Use

- Depletion of resources for satisfying the greed of a few individuals.
    
- Accumulation of resources in a few hands, dividing society into rich and poor.
    
- Global ecological crises (e.g., global warming, ozone layer depletion, environmental pollution, land degradation).
    

### Sustainable Development

- **Definition:** Development that meets current needs without damaging the environment and without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
    
- **Rio de Janeiro Earth Summit (1992):** Over 100 heads of state met in Brazil and adopted **Agenda 21** to achieve global sustainable development, fight environmental damage, poverty, and disease through global cooperation.
    

## 4. Resource Planning in India

Resource planning is necessary for balanced development in a country with diverse resource availability.

### Three Steps of Resource Planning

1. **Identification and Inventory:** Surveying, mapping, qualitative and quantitative estimation, and measurement of resources.
    
2. **Planning Structure:** Evolving a development framework endowed with appropriate technology, skill, and institutional setup.
    
3. **National Matching:** Matching resource development plans with overall national development plans.
    

## 5. Conservation of Resources

- Over-utilization and irrational consumption lead to socio-economic and environmental problems.
    
- **Gandhi's View:** _"There is enough for everybody's need, but not for anybody's greed."_ He blamed greedy individuals and modern technology for resource depletion and favored mass production by masses.
    

## 6. Land Resources & Land Use Pattern in India

Land is a natural resource supporting economic activities, vegetation, wildlife, and infrastructure.

### Relief Features of India

- **Plains (43%):** Provide facilities for agriculture and industry.
    
- **Mountains (30%):** Ensure perennial flow of rivers, provide facilities for tourism and ecological balance.
    
- **Plateaus (27%):** Rich reserves of minerals, fossil fuels, and forests.
    

## 7. Land Degradation and Conservation Measures

### Causes of Land Degradation

- **Deforestation** and overgrazing (e.g., Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh).
    
- **Mining and Quarrying:** Leaves deep scars and traces of over-burdening (e.g., Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Odisha).
    
- **Over-Irrigation:** Leads to waterlogging, increasing salinity and alkalinity in soil (e.g., Punjab, Haryana, Western UP).
    
- **Industrial Effluents:** Pollute land and water resources.
    

### Conservation Measures

- Afforestation and proper management of grazing lands.
    
- Planting of **shelterbelts** for plants.
    
- Stabilization of sand dunes by growing thorny bushes.
    
- Proper treatment and management of industrial effluents before discharge.

## 9. Soil Erosion and Conservation

- **Soil Erosion:** The denudation and washing away of the soil cover.
    
    - **Gully Erosion:** Running water cuts through clayey soils, forming deep channels (gullies). Bad land unfit for cultivation is called **ravines** (e.g., Chambal basin).
        
    - **Sheet Erosion:** Topsoil washed away over large areas by water flowing as a sheet down a slope.
        

### Soil Conservation Techniques

1. **Contour Ploughing:** Ploughing along contour lines decelerates water flow down slopes.
    
2. **Terrace Farming:** Steps cut out on slopes create terraces, restricting erosion (common in Western and Central Himalayas).
    
3. **Strip Cropping:** Large fields divided into strips; strips of grass are left to grow between crops to break wind force.
    
4. **Shelterbelts:** Rows of trees planted to create shelter, stabilizing sand dunes in deserts.
