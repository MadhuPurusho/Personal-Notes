# Class 10 English Grammar | Board Exam Notes

## Chapter: Determiners
*CBSE English Language and Literature (Grammar Section)*

> **Core Concept:** A **Determiner** is a word placed before a noun to limit, specify, or clarify its meaning. It points out *which one*, *whose*, *how much*, or *how many*. Unlike descriptive adjectives, determiners do not describe traits; they fix the reference of the noun.

---

## Story Overview & Key Categories

### Major Categories of Determiners

| Category | Definition / Role | Key Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Articles** | Specifies whether a noun is indefinite (general) or definite (specific). | *a, an, the* |
| **Demonstratives** | Points out specific nouns based on proximity (distance/time). | *this, that, these, those* |
| **Possessives** | Indicates ownership or relationship. | *my, your, his, her, its, our, their* |
| **Quantifiers** | Indicates quantity or amount (countable and uncountable). | *some, any, much, many, little, few, several* |
| **Distributives** | Refers to individual elements or members of a group. | *each, every, either, neither* |
| **Interrogatives** | Used before nouns to ask questions. | *which, what, whose* |
| **Cardinals & Ordinals** | Indicates exact number count or position in a series. | *one, two, first, second, next, last* |

---

## Comprehensive Breakdown & Rules

### 1. Articles (A / An / The)

#### A. Indefinite Articles: *A / An*
- Used with **singular countable nouns** introduced for the first time.
- **Rule of Sound (Crucial for Boards):** Selection depends on **initial phonetic sound**, NOT the written letter.
  - Use **A** before a consonant sound: *a university* ($\text{/juː/}$ sound), *a European*, *a one-rupee coin* ($\text{/wʌn/}$ sound), *a useful book*.
  - Use **An** before a vowel sound: *an hour* (silent 'h'), *an honest man*, *an M.A. degree* ($\text{/em/}$ sound), *an umbrella*.

#### B. Definite Article: *The*
- Used before **singular/plural countable** and **uncountable nouns** when specific.
- **Key Uses:**
  1. Nouns mentioned second time: *I saw a boy. **The** boy was crying.*
  2. Unique entities: *the sun*, *the moon*, *the earth*.
  3. Superlatives: *He is **the** best student in class.*
  4. Rivers, Oceans, Mountain Ranges, Sacred Books: *The Ganga*, *The Himalayas*, *The Gita*.
- **Omission of 'The' (Zero Article):**
  - Do NOT use before proper nouns, abstract nouns, material nouns used generally, languages, or sports: *Gold is valuable* (Not: *The gold...*), *He plays cricket*.

---

### 2. Demonstratives & Possessives

This / These  --->  Near in location / time
That / Those  --->  Distant in location / time

- **Demonstratives:** 
  - *This / That* + Singular Noun: *this chair*, *that building*.
  - *These / Those* + Plural Noun: *these books*, *those boys*.
- **Possessives:** Show ownership directly before a noun.
  - *my*, *your*, *his*, *her*, *its*, *our*, *their*.
  - *Distinction:* **Possessive Determiner** (*This is **my** book*) vs **Possessive Pronoun** (*This book is **mine***).

---

### 3. High-Yield Quantifiers (Frequently Tested)

#### A. Little / A Little / The Little (Uncountable Nouns)
1. **Little:** Negligible / Almost none (Negative meaning).
2. **A Little:** Some amount, though small (Positive meaning).
3. **The Little:** All that small amount which is available.

#### B. Few / A Few / The Few (Plural Countable Nouns)
1. **Few:** Hardly any / Almost none (Negative meaning).
2. **A Few:** A small number (Positive meaning).
3. **The Few:** All of that small number present.

#### C. Some vs. Any
- **Some:** Used in **affirmative sentences** and **offers/requests** expecting a 'yes'.
  - *There is **some** water in the jug.*
  - *Would you like **some** tea?*
- **Any:** Used in **negative sentences** and **interrogative sentences**.
  - *There isn't **any** water left.*
  - *Have you got **any** money?*

#### D. Much vs. Many
- **Much:** Used with **uncountable nouns**. (*Much patience*, *much work*)
- **Many:** Used with **plural countable nouns**. (*Many students*, *many options*)

---

### 4. Distributives (Each, Every, Either, Neither)

1. **Each:** Refers to individual items in a small, definite group (two or more). Taken singular.
   - *Example:* **Each** student received a prize.
2. **Every:** Refers to all members of a large, indefinite group as a whole. Taken singular.
   - *Example:* **Every** citizen must respect the law.
3. **Either:** "One or the other" of two items.
   - *Example:* You can take **either** side of the road.
4. **Neither:** "Not one nor the other" of two items (Negative).
   - *Example:* **Neither** statement is true.

## Board Exam Editing & Gap-Filling Rules Summary

| Determiner Type | Noun Compatibility | Example Usage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A / An** | Singular Countable Nouns | *a car*, *an orange* |
| **Much, A little, Little** | Uncountable Nouns | *much effort*, *a little sugar* |
| **Many, A few, Few, Several** | Plural Countable Nouns | *many books*, *several days* |
| **Some, Any, The, All** | Countable & Uncountable Nouns | *some milk*, *some apples* |

## Important Practice Questions & Model Answers

### Q1: Fill in the blanks with suitable determiners.
1. *He is ________ honest officer, but his brother is ________ university student.*
2. *There was ________ milk left in the vessel, so she couldn't prepare tea.*
3. *________ member of the team was awarded a medal.*

> **Answers:**
> 1. **an** *(vowel sound /ɒ/)*, **a** *(consonant sound /j/)*
> 2. **little** *(implies almost no milk - negative context)*
> 3. **Each** *(refers to individual members of a specific team)*

### Q2: Error Correction (Board Exam Pattern)

1. **Incorrect:** *He has many knowledge about the topic.*
   - **Correct:** *He has **much** (or **a lot of**) knowledge about the topic.* *(Reason: Knowledge is uncountable)*.
2. **Incorrect:** *A few money was stolen yesterday.*
   - **Correct:** ***Some** (or **A little**) money was stolen yesterday.* *(Reason: Money is uncountable; 'few' is for countable nouns)*.

---

## Summary / Key Takeaways

- **Sound Check for Articles:** Always check the initial sound, not the spelling (*an honest*, *a European*).
- **Countable vs. Uncountable Distinction:** Never mix quantifiers (*few/many* for countable, *little/much* for uncountable).
- **Subject-Verb Agreement Link:** Distributives (*each, every, either, neither*) take a **singular noun and singular verb**.
