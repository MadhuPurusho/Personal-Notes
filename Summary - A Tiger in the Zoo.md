### 1. Poem Overview

**Poet:** Leslie Norris  
**Genre:** Descriptive / Philosophical Poem  
**Structure:** 5 Stanzas (4 lines each - Quatrains)  
**Rhyme Scheme:** `abcb` (for all stanzas)

> **Core Theme:** The cruel contrast between captivity and freedom. The poem highlights the helplessness, anger, and loss of dignity of a wild animal confined in a cage compared to its natural, majestic existence in the wild.

---

### 2. Complete Text of the Poem

```text
He stalks in his vivid stripes
The few steps of his cage,
On pads of velvet quiet,
In his quiet rage.

He should be lurking in shadow,
Sliding through long grass
Near the water hole
Where plump deer pass.

He should be snarling around houses
At the jungle’s edge,
Baring his white fangs, his claws,
Terrorising the village!

But he’s locked in a concrete cell,
His strength behind bars,
Stalking the length of his cage,
Ignoring visitors.

He hears the last voice at night,
The patrolling cars,
And stares with his brilliant eyes
At the brilliant stars.
```
### 3. Stanza-by-Stanza Detailed Analysis

#### Stanza 1: The Tiger in the Cage

- **Lines:** _"He stalks in his vivid stripes / The few steps of his cage, / On pads of velvet quiet, / In his quiet rage."_
    
- **Explanation:** The tiger walks majestically (_"stalks"_) inside his small cage, showing off his bright, clear stripes (_"vivid stripes"_). His movement is restricted to just a few steps due to the limited space. He steps softly on his soft paws (_"pads of velvet quiet"_), hiding a deep, suppressed anger (_"quiet rage"_) at being imprisoned.
    

#### Stanza 2: The Tiger in his Natural Habitat (Forest)

- **Lines:** _"He should be lurking in shadow, / Sliding through long grass / Near the water hole / Where plump deer pass."_
    
- **Explanation:** The poet imagines what the tiger _should_ be doing if he were free. He ought to be hiding in the shadows of trees, moving silently through tall grass near a water body, waiting patiently to hunt healthy deer (_"plump deer"_).
    

#### Stanza 3: Natural Behavior at the Jungle's Edge

- **Lines:** _"He should be snarling around houses / At the jungle’s edge, / Baring his white fangs, his claws, / Terrorising the village!"_
    
- **Explanation:** If free, the tiger would roam near human settlements on the border of the forest. He would growl, showing his sharp white teeth (_"fangs"_) and claws, asserting his natural dominance and instilling awe and fear in the villagers.
    

#### Stanza 4: Back to the Reality of Captivity

- **Lines:** _"But he’s locked in a concrete cell, / His strength behind bars, / Stalking the length of his cage, / Ignoring visitors."_
    
- **Explanation:** The poem returns to the grim reality. The tiger is locked in a strong cell made of concrete, and his immense power is rendered useless behind iron bars. He walks back and forth across the cage, completely indifferent to the visitors who come to gaze at him.
    

#### Stanza 5: The Solitary Night

- **Lines:** _"He hears the last voice at night, / The patrolling cars, / And stares with his brilliant eyes / At the brilliant stars."_
    
- **Explanation:** At night, surrounded by solitude, the tiger hears the noise of guards in patrolling cars. He gazes with his bright, shining eyes at the brilliant stars in the night sky—his only connection to the vast, free natural world he belongs to.
    

### 4. Contrast: Cage vs. Wild

|**Feature / Aspect**|**In the Cage (Captivity)**|**In the Wild (Freedom)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Movement**|Restricted to a few steps in a concrete cell.|Moves freely through long grass, shadows, and open territory.|
|**Prowess & Power**|Suppressed, helpless, "strength behind bars."|Feared, hunting deer, terrorizing villages.|
|**Attitude**|Indifferent, ignores visitors, holds "quiet rage."|Active, hunting, roaring, baring fangs.|
|**Environment**|Artificial concrete walls, noise of patrolling cars.|Natural habitat—forests, water holes, open skies.|

### 5. Literary & Poetic Devices

1. **Personification:** The poet uses the pronoun **"He"** instead of "it" for the tiger, giving him human feelings of dignity and rage.
    
2. **Metaphor:**
    
    - _"Pads of velvet quiet"_ – The tiger's soft paws are directly compared to soft velvet.
        
3. **Oxymoron:**
    
    - _"Quiet rage"_ – Two opposite words placed together ("quiet" and "rage") to convey suppressed anger.
        
4. **Alliteration:** Repetition of initial consonant sounds:
    
    - _"**B**ehind **b**ars"_ (`/b/` sound)
        
    - _"**H**e **h**ears"_ (`/h/` sound)
        
    - _"**P**lump deer **p**ass"_ (`/p/` sound)
        
5. **Imagery:**
    
    - **Visual:** _"vivid stripes"_, _"concrete cell"_, _"brilliant stars"_, _"white fangs"_
        
    - **Auditory:** _"snarling"_, _"last voice at night"_
        
6. **Repetition:** Used for poetic emphasis:
    
    - _"Quiet"_ / _"Quiet rage"_
        
    - _"Brilliant eyes"_ / _"Brilliant stars"_
        
7. **Enjambment:** Continuation of sentences across line breaks without punctuation (e.g., Stanza 2 and Stanza 3).

#### Q2. Notice the use of a word repeated in lines such as these:

1. _On pads of velvet quiet, / In his quiet rage._
    
2. _And stares with his brilliant eyes / At the brilliant stars._
    

**What is the effect of this repetition?**

**Answer:** The poet uses repetition to heighten emotional intensity and create poetic contrast:

- **"Quiet" / "Quiet rage":** Emphasizes the tiger's helpless situation. His steps are quiet because of his soft paws, but his rage is quiet because he cannot express his anger or escape.
    
- **"Brilliant eyes" / "Brilliant stars":** Connects the tiger's inner spark and longing for freedom with the endless, shining universe outside his cage, expressing a silent hope for liberty.
    

### 7. Summary & Key Takeaways

- **Moral Message:** Wild animals belong in their natural habitats. Keeping them caged for human entertainment robs them of their freedom, strength, and natural instincts.
    
- **Emotional Contrast:** The poem shifts masterfully between the artificial confinement of the zoo and the vibrant freedom of the forest.
    
- **Poetic Brilliance:** Through devices like oxymoron (_"quiet rage"_) and repetition (_"brilliant"_), Leslie Norris creates a deep, empathetic portrait of a caged animal.
