## 1. Acids and Bases

### Basic Comparison

| **Property**      | **Acids**                                                                | **Bases**                                  |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Taste**         | Sour                                                                     | Bitter                                     |
| **Litmus Test**   | Turns Blue Litmus $\rightarrow$ Red                                      | Turns Red Litmus $\rightarrow$ Blue        |
| **Ions in Water** | Releases Hydrogen/Hydronium ions ($\text{H}^+$ / $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$) | Releases Hydroxide ions ($\text{OH}^-$)    |
| **Touch**         | —                                                                        | Soapy or slippery to touch                 |
| **Examples**      | $\text{HCl}, \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4, \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$              | $\text{NaOH}, \text{KOH}, \text{Ca(OH)}_2$ |

> **Note on Alkalis:** Water-soluble bases are called **alkalis** (e.g., $\text{NaOH}$, $\text{KOH}$). All alkalis are bases, but not all bases are alkalis.

### Indicators

Indicators are substances that change their color or odor in acidic or basic mediums.

1. **Natural Indicators:**
    
    - **Litmus:** Acid $\rightarrow$ Red | Base $\rightarrow$ Blue
        
    - **Turmeric:** Acid $\rightarrow$ Yellow | Base $\rightarrow$ Reddish-brown
        
2. **Synthetic Indicators:**
    
    - **Phenolphthalein:** Acid $\rightarrow$ Colorless | Base $\rightarrow$ Pink
        
    - **Methyl Orange:** Acid $\rightarrow$ Red | Base $\rightarrow$ Yellow
        
3. **Olfactory Indicators:** Change smell in acidic or basic solutions (e.g., onion, vanilla essence, clove oil).
    

## 2. Chemical Properties of Acids and Bases

### 1. Reaction with Metals

- **Acid + Metal $\longrightarrow$ Salt + Hydrogen Gas ($\text{H}_2\uparrow$)**
    
    $$\text{Zn}(s) + 2\text{HCl}(aq) \longrightarrow \text{ZnCl}_2(aq) + \text{H}_2(g)\uparrow$$
    
    _(Hydrogen gas burns with a popping sound when a burning candle is brought near it.)_
    
- **Base + Metal $\longrightarrow$ Salt + Hydrogen Gas ($\text{H}_2\uparrow$)**
    
    $$2\text{NaOH}(aq) + \text{Zn}(s) \longrightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{ZnO}_2(aq) + \text{H}_2(g)\uparrow$$
    
    _$\text{Na}_2\text{ZnO}_2$: Sodium Zincate_
    

### 2. Reaction of Metal Carbonates / Hydrogen Carbonates with Acids

- **Metal Carbonate / Bicarbonate + Acid $\longrightarrow$ Salt + Water + Carbon Dioxide ($\text{CO}_2\uparrow$)**
    
    $$\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3(s) + 2\text{HCl}(aq) \longrightarrow 2\text{NaCl}(aq) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) + \text{CO}_2(g)\uparrow$$
    
    $$\text{NaHCO}_3(s) + \text{HCl}(aq) \longrightarrow \text{NaCl}(aq) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) + \text{CO}_2(g)\uparrow$$
    

> **Lime Water Test for $\text{CO}_2$ Gas:**
> 
> Passing $\text{CO}_2$ through lime water turns it milky due to insoluble calcium carbonate:
> 
> $$\text{Ca(OH)}_2(aq) + \text{CO}_2(g) \longrightarrow \text{CaCO}_3(s)\downarrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$$
> 
> On passing **excess** $\text{CO}_2$, milkiness disappears as soluble calcium hydrogen carbonate is formed:
> 
> $$\text{CaCO}_3(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) + \text{CO}_2(g) \longrightarrow \text{Ca(HCO}_3)_2(aq)$$

### 3. Neutralization Reaction (Acid + Base)

The reaction between an acid and a base to form salt and water.

$$\text{Acid} + \text{Base} \longrightarrow \text{Salt} + \text{Water}$$

$$\text{HCl}(aq) + \text{NaOH}(aq) \longrightarrow \text{NaCl}(aq) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$$

### 4. Reaction of Metallic & Non-Metallic Oxides

- **Metal Oxides are Basic:** React with acids to form salt and water.
    
    $$\text{CuO}(s) + 2\text{HCl}(aq) \longrightarrow \text{CuCl}_2(aq) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$$
    
    _(Copper chloride gives a blue-green color to the solution)._
    
- **Non-Metal Oxides are Acidic:** React with bases to form salt and water.
    
    $$\text{Ca(OH)}_2(aq) + \text{CO}_2(g) \longrightarrow \text{CaCO}_3(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$$
    

## 3. The pH Scale & Strength of Solutions

The **pH Scale** measures the concentration of hydrogen ions ($\text{H}^+$) in a solution, ranging from 0 to 14.

```
           Acidic Nature Increases          Basic Nature Increases
       ◄─────────────────────────── 7 ───────────────────────────►
  0                                 │                                14
[High H⁺]                         Neutral                         [High OH⁻]
```

- **$\text{pH} < 7$:** Acidic solution
    
- **$\text{pH} = 7$:** Neutral solution
    
- **$\text{pH} > 7$:** Basic solution
    

### Importance of pH in Daily Life

1. **In Human Body:** Works within a narrow pH range of $7.0\text{ to }7.8$.
    
2. **Acid Rain:** Rainwater with a pH lower than $5.6$.
    
3. **In Digestion:** The stomach produces $\text{HCl}$ ($\text{pH} \approx 1.2$). Excess acid causes acidity, treated using **antacids** like Mild Bases ($\text{Mg(OH)}_2$ - Milk of Magnesia).
    
4. **Tooth Decay:** Starts when mouth pH falls below $5.5$. Enamel ($\text{Ca}_5(\text{PO}_4)_3\text{OH}$) gets corroded by acids produced by mouth bacteria.
    
5. **Self-Defense:** Bee stings inject methanoic acid (formic acid), causing pain. Treating it with baking soda gives relief.

### Water of Crystallization

The fixed number of water molecules present in one formula unit of a salt.

- **Gypsum:** $\text{CaSO}_4\cdot2\text{H}_2\text{O}$
    
- **Blue Vitriol (Copper Sulphate):** $\text{CuSO}_4\cdot5\text{H}_2\text{O}$
    
- **Washing Soda:** $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3\cdot10\text{H}_2\text{O}$
