## 1. Introduction to Control and Coordination

- **Stimulus:** Any change in the external or internal environment to which an organism responds (e.g., light, heat, cold, sound, touch).
- **Response:** The reaction or action shown by an organism in response to a stimulus.
- **Coordination:** The process through which two or more organs interact and complement the functions of one another.
- In animals, control and coordination take place via two systems:
  1. **Nervous System** (Fast, electrical impulse-based)
  2. **Endocrine System** (Slower, chemical messenger-based)

---

## 2. Animals – Nervous System

### 2.1 Receptors
Specialized cells or tissue tips located in sense organs that detect stimuli from the environment:
- **Photoreceptors:** Detect light (Eyes)
- **Phonoreceptors:** Detect sound/hearing and balance (Ears)
- **Olfactory Receptors:** Detect smell (Nose)
- **Gustatory Receptors:** Detect taste (Tongue)
- **Thermoreceptors / Tangoreceptors:** Detect temperature and touch (Skin)

---

### 2.2 Structure of a Neuron
The **Neuron** is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system.

> [!info] Parts of a Neuron
> - **Dendrites:** Short, branched structures that collect information/stimuli and convert them into electrical impulses.
> - **Cyton (Cell Body):** Contains the nucleus and cytoplasm; processes the signal.
> - **Axon:** Longest fiber that carries the electrical impulse away from the cell body to the axon terminals.
> - **Synapse:** The microscopic gap between the axon terminal of one neuron and the dendrites of the next.

#### Transmission of Nerve Impulses:
1. Stimulus triggers a chemical reaction at the dendrite tip, generating an **electrical impulse**.
2. The impulse travels: **Dendrite -> Cell Body -> Axon -> Axon Terminal**.
3. At the **synapse**, the impulse triggers the release of chemicals (**neurotransmitters**), which cross the gap and generate a new electrical impulse in the next neuron.
4. **Neuromuscular Junction (NMJ):** The point where a motor neuron meets a muscle fiber.

---

### 2.3 Reflex Action and Reflex Arc

- **Reflex Action:** A quick, sudden, and involuntary response of the body to an environmental stimulus (e.g., pulling back hand after touching a hot object).
- **Reflex Arc:** The pathway taken by electrical impulses during a reflex action.

Stimulus -> Receptor -> Sensory Neuron -> Spinal Cord (Relay Neuron) -> Motor Neuron -> Effector (Muscle/Gland) -> Response

> [!note] Significance of Reflex Arc
> It bypasses the main thinking process of the brain to enable faster responses and protect the body from potential harm.

---

### 2.4 Human Brain
The **Central Nervous System (CNS)** consists of the **Brain** and **Spinal Cord**. The brain is protected by:
- A bony box (**Cranium/Skull**).
- Three protective membranes (**Meninges**).
- **Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF)** acting as a shock absorber.

#### A. Forebrain
- Main **thinking part** of the brain.
- **Cerebrum:** Largest part; controls voluntary actions, memory, intelligence, reasoning, emotions, and receives sensory inputs (sight, hearing, taste, smell).
- **Hypothalamus:** Regulates body temperature, hunger, thirst, and controls the pituitary gland.

#### B. Midbrain
- Connects the forebrain to the hindbrain.
- Controls involuntary actions and visual/auditory reflex movements (e.g., eye pupil size adjustment).

#### C. Hindbrain
- **Cerebellum:** Controls posture, balance, and precision of voluntary movements (e.g., walking in a straight line, riding a bicycle).
- **Pons:** Regulates respiratory rate and relays impulses.
- **Medulla Oblongata:** Controls involuntary activities like heartbeat, blood pressure, salivation, vomiting, and swallowing.

---

### 2.5 Peripheral & Autonomic Nervous System
- **Peripheral Nervous System (PNS):**
  - **Cranial Nerves:** 12 pairs originating from the brain.
  - **Spinal Nerves:** 31 pairs originating from the spinal cord.
- **How Tissue Effects Action:** Nerve impulse reaches muscle -> Muscle cells change shape using special proteins -> Muscle contracts or relaxes.

## 3. Coordination in Plants

Plants lack a nervous system and muscles; they coordinate via chemical signals (**Phytohormones**).

### 3.1 Plant Movements

Plant movements are divided into two primary types:

#### 1. Tropic Movements (Directional Growth Movements)
Growth movements dependent on the direction of the stimulus.

| Movement Type | Stimulus | Positive Response | Negative Response |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phototropism** | Light | Shoots grow towards light | Roots grow away from light |
| **Geotropism** | Gravity | Roots grow downwards | Shoots grow upwards |
| **Hydrotropism** | Water | Roots grow towards water | Shoots grow away |
| **Chemotropism** | Chemicals | Growth of pollen tube towards ovule | - |
| **Thigmotropism** | Touch | Tendrils coiling around a support | - |

#### 2. Nastic Movements (Non-directional / Immediate Response)
- Non-growth, independent-of-direction movements caused by changes in turgor pressure (water content) in cells.
- **Example:** Folding of leaves in *Mimosa pudica* ("Touch-me-not") upon being touched (**Thigmonasty**).

---

### 3.2 Plant Hormones (Phytohormones)

#### A. Growth Promoters
- **Auxins:** Synthesized at shoot tips. Promotes cell elongation and controls phototropism (accumulates on the shady side of the stem, causing cells to grow longer on that side).
- **Gibberellins:** Promotes stem elongation, seed germination, and flowering.
- **Cytokinins:** Promotes cell division; present in high concentrations in areas of rapid cell division (e.g., fruits, seeds).

#### B. Growth Inhibitors
- **Abscisic Acid (ABA):** Inhibits growth, induces seed dormancy, causes wilting and falling of leaves, promotes stomatal closure during stress.

---

## 4. Hormones in Animals (Endocrine System)

Endocrine glands are **ductless glands** that secrete chemical messengers called **hormones** directly into the bloodstream.

### Major Human Endocrine Glands & Functions

| Gland | Location | Hormone Secreted | Function / Clinical Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pituitary** | Base of Brain | Growth Hormone (GH) | Regulates growth/development. Deficiency -> Dwarfism; Excess -> Gigantism. |
| **Thyroid** | Neck / Throat region | Thyroxine | Regulates carbohydrate, fat, and protein metabolism. Requires **Iodine** for synthesis (Deficiency -> **Goitre**). |
| **Pancreas** | Below Stomach | Insulin & Glucagon | **Insulin** lowers blood sugar levels (Deficiency -> **Diabetes mellitus**). |
| **Adrenal** | Top of Kidneys | Adrenaline | "Emergency/Stress hormone" — increases heart rate, breathing rate, and blood pressure during fight-or-flight scenarios. |
| **Testes** (Male) | Scrotum | Testosterone | Controls male secondary sexual characters and sperm production. |
| **Ovaries** (Female) | Lower Abdomen | Estrogen & Progesterone | Controls female secondary sexual characters, menstrual cycle, and pregnancy support. |

---

### 4.1 Feedback Mechanism
- System that regulates the timing and quantity of hormone release in the body.
- **Example (Blood Sugar Control):**
  High blood glucose -> Pancreatic cells detect it -> Insulin secreted -> Blood glucose drops -> Insulin secretion reduced

---

## 5. Key Concept Map Summary

- [[Control and Coordination]] relies on both **Electrical** (Nervous) and **Chemical** (Hormonal) paths.
- **Electrical signals** are rapid but short-lived and cannot reach every cell.
- **Chemical signals** reach all cells via the bloodstream/fluids, acting steadily with longer-lasting effects.
