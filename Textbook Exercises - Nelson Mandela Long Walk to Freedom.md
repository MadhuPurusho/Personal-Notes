## 1. Oral Comprehension Check (OCC)

### Page 18

#### Q1. Where did the ceremonies take place? Can you name any public buildings in India made of sandstone?
**Answer:** The ceremonies took place in the sandstone amphitheatre formed by the Union Buildings in Pretoria.  
In India, notable public buildings made of sandstone include the **Rashtrapati Bhavan**, the **Parliament House (Sansad Bhavan)**, and the **Red Fort** in New Delhi.

#### Q2. Can you say how 10 May is an 'autumn day' in South Africa?
**Answer:** South Africa is located in the Southern Hemisphere, where the seasons are opposite to those in the Northern Hemisphere. May falls during the autumn season in the Southern Hemisphere, making 10 May an "autumn day."

#### Q3. At the beginning of his speech, Mandela mentions "an extraordinary human disaster". What does he mean by this? What is the "glorious... human achievement" he speaks of at the end?
**Answer:** 
* **"An extraordinary human disaster"** refers to the oppressive system of **Apartheid** in South Africa, under which the dark-skinned population suffered severe racial discrimination, total lack of rights, and brutal oppression by the white minority for decades.
* **"Glorious human achievement"** refers to the establishment of South Africa’s first democratic, non-racial government, granting equal rights, freedom, and dignity to all citizens regardless of their race.

#### Q4. What does Mandela thank the international leaders for?
**Answer:** Mandela thanks the international leaders for coming to celebrate the historic victory of South Africa's independence. He views their presence as a joint triumph for justice, peace, and human dignity, signaling the end of South Africa's global isolation.

#### Q5. What ideals does he set out for the future of South Africa?
**Answer:** Mandela pledges to liberate all South Africans from the continuing bondage of **poverty, deprivation, suffering, gender bias, and other forms of discrimination**. He promises that the beautiful land of South Africa will never again experience the oppression of one group by another.

### Page 21

#### Q1. What do the military generals do? How has their attitude changed, and why?
**Answer:** The highest military generals and police officers saluted Nelson Mandela and pledged their loyalty to the new democratic government.  
Their attitude changed because South Africa had transitioned from an oppressive white-minority regime to a non-racial democracy. Years prior, these same generals would have arrested Mandela as an outlaw, but now they recognized him as their elected Commander-in-Chief.

#### Q2. Why were two national anthems sung?
**Answer:** Two national anthems were sung to symbolize the equality, reconciliation, and unity of the two major racial groups in South Africa:
1. **'Nkosi Sikelel’ iAfrika'** – The anthem traditionally sung by the Black community.
2. **'Die Stem'** – The old national anthem sung by the White community.

#### Q3. How does Mandela describe the systems of government in his country—
1. **In the first decade of the twentieth century?**
2. **In the final decade of the twentieth century?**

**Answer:**
1. **First Decade:** Following the Anglo-Boer War, white South Africans patched up their differences and erected a system of racial domination against the dark-skinned peoples of their land, establishing the harsh system known as **Apartheid**.
2. **Final Decade:** The brutal system of racial domination was overturned forever and replaced by a system that recognized the equal rights and freedoms of all human beings, regardless of the color of their skin.

#### Q4. What does courage mean to Mandela?
**Answer:** To Mandela, **courage is not the absence of fear, but the triumph over it**. A brave man is not someone who does not feel afraid, but someone who conquers that fear.

#### Q5. Which does he think is natural, to love or to hate?
**Answer:** Mandela believes that **love comes more naturally** to the human heart than hatred. Since hatred is learned, people can also be taught to love.

### Page 24

#### Q1. What "twin obligations" does Mandela mention?
**Answer:** Mandela mentions that every man has two primary obligations:
1. **First Obligation (Private/Personal):** To his family, parents, wife, and children.
2. **Second Obligation (Public/Community):** To his people, his community, and his country.

#### Q2. What did being free mean to Mandela as a boy, and as a student? How does he contrast these "transitory freedoms" with the "basic and honorable freedoms"?
**Answer:**
* **As a Boy:** Freedom meant physical liberty—running in the fields, swimming in clear streams, roasting mealies under the stars, and riding sluggish bulls.
* **As a Student:** Freedom meant "transitory freedoms"—being able to stay out late at night, reading what he pleased, and going where he chose.
* **Contrast:** Transitory freedoms were temporary, selfish, and individual desires. In contrast, "basic and honorable freedoms" involved achieving full potential, earning a living, marrying, having a family, and securing equal rights for his community without state interference.

#### Q3. Does Mandela think the oppressor is free? Why/Why not?
**Answer:** No, Mandela believes that the oppressor is **not free**. He asserts that a person who deprives another of their freedom is a prisoner of hatred, locked behind the bars of prejudice and narrow-mindedness. Both the oppressor and the oppressed are robbed of their humanity.

## 2. Textual Questions (Thinking about the Text)

#### Q1. Why did such a large number of international leaders attend the inauguration? What did it signify the triumph of?
**Answer:** International leaders attended the inauguration to support the end of Apartheid and mark the official birth of a democratic, non-racial South Africa. It signified the global triumph of **justice, peace, and human dignity** over racial oppression and isolation.

#### Q2. What does Mandela mean when he says he is "simply the sum of all those African patriots" who had gone before him?
**Answer:** Mandela humbly acknowledges that the victory of democracy was not his alone. He represents the cumulative sacrifices, courage, and struggles of thousands of anti-apartheid freedom fighters who came before him and paved the way for liberty without living to see the final triumph.

#### Q3. Would you agree that the "depths of oppression" create "heights of character"? How does Mandela illustrate this? Can you add your own example to this thought?
**Answer:** Yes, extreme suffering and systemic oppression often forge unparalleled mental resilience, nobility, and bravery.  
* **Mandela's Illustration:** He points out that the brutal oppression of Apartheid produced figures of extraordinary courage, wisdom, and generosity like Oliver Tambo, Walter Sisulu, Chief Luthuli, and Yusuf Dadoo.
* **Additional Example:** In India's freedom struggle, centuries of British colonial rule gave rise to stalwart leaders of incredible character, such as Mahatma Gandhi, Bhagat Singh, and Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose.

#### Q4. How did Mandela's understanding of freedom change with age and experience?
**Answer:** 
1. **Childhood:** Freedom was perceived as simple physical liberty (running, swimming, playing).
2. **Youth/Student:** Freedom meant personal autonomy (staying out late, choosing books).
3. **Adulthood:** He realized his personal freedoms were an illusion because all Black citizens were stripped of basic civil rights. This expanded his definition of freedom to encompass the collective dignity and self-respect of his entire community.

#### Q5. How did Mandela's 'hunger for freedom' change his life?
**Answer:** The desire for his people's freedom transformed Mandela from a law-abiding attorney into a bold political rebel. It forced him to leave his comfortable home and family, live like an outlaw, and dedicate 30 years of his life to imprisonment until total liberation was achieved.

### Concept 1: Noun Formation from Verbs & Adjectives

In English, abstract nouns are often derived by adding specific suffixes (such as `-tion`, `-ment`, `-ty`, `-ance`, `-ence`) to verbs and adjectives.

| Verb / Adjective | Derived Abstract Noun |
| :--- | :--- |
| Rebel | Rebellion |
| Obligate | Obligation |
| Transform | Transformation |
| Equal | Equality |
| Assist | Assistance |
| Resilient | Resilience |

### Concept 2: The Definite Article ('The') with Proper Nouns

Generally, proper nouns (names of people or places) do not take the definite article **'the'**. However, **'the'** is placed before a proper noun in specific exception cases:

1. **Uniqueness / Distinction:** When referring to a unique person or quality (*"the Nelson Mandela of our era"*).
2. **Plural Names / Groups:** When referring to families or plural place names (*"the Tambos"*, *"the United States"*).
3. **Qualifying Clauses:** When a proper noun is defined by an adjective clause (*"the Mandela who fought for justice"*).

#### Textual Exercise:
*Insert the definite article 'the' where necessary:*

1. **Question:** Mr. Singh is **a** Newton of our school.  
   **Correction:** Mr. Singh is **the** Newton of our school. *(Used because he is compared to Isaac Newton).*
2. **Question:** We visited **the** Union Buildings in Pretoria. *(Correct as stated: refers to a specific public landmark).*
