## 1. What Development Promises – Different People, Different Goals

- **Varied Goals:** Different people have different development goals because their life situations and needs are different.
    
- **Conflicting Goals:** What may be development for one person may not be development for another; it may even be destructive for the other.
    
    - _Example:_ A dam constructed for electricity benefits industrial consumers, but displaces local tribal communities whose land gets submerged.
        

## 2. Income and Other Goals

While higher income is a central development goal, people also seek non-material aspects that improve quality of life:

- **Equal treatment** and non-discrimination
    
- **Freedom** and personal liberty
    
- **Security** of life and job
    
- **Respect** from others and dignity
    
- **Mixed Goals:** For development, people look at a mix of goals rather than income alone.
    

## 3. [[National Development]]

- National development refers to the capacity of a country to improve the social, economic, and political well-being of its citizens.
    
- Different individuals can have different as well as conflicting notions of what a country's development should be.
    

## 4. How to Compare Different Countries or States?

Countries are classified into developed, developing, or underdeveloped using economic and human indicators.

### A. [[Average Income]] / Per Capita Income

To compare countries, total income is divided by total population:

$$\text{Per Capita Income} = \frac{\text{Total Income of the Country}}{\text{Total Population}}$$

- **World Bank Criterion (World Development Reports):**
    
    - **High-Income / Rich Countries:** Countries with per capita income above a specific threshold set annually by the World Bank.
        
    - **Low-Income Countries:** Countries below the minimum income threshold.
        
- **Limitations of Average Income:**
    
    - Hides **disparities** in income distribution. It does not show how income is divided among rich and poor populations.
        

### B. Income and Other Criteria (Comparing Indian States)

When comparing states (e.g., Punjab/Haryana, Kerala, Bihar), income alone does not give a complete picture. Other socio-economic indicators are essential:

|**Indicator**|**Definition**|**Significance**|
|---|---|---|
|**Infant Mortality Rate (IMR)**|Number of children who die before age 1 per 1,000 live births in a year.|Indicates healthcare facilities and maternal care.|
|**Literacy Rate**|Proportion of literate population in the 7+ age group.|Measures access to basic education.|
|**Net Attendance Ratio**|Percentage of children (ages 14–15) attending school out of total children in that age group.|Reflects secondary education participation.|

> **Key Example:** Kerala has lower per capita income than some other states, but it ranks significantly higher on Human Development due to better healthcare, public distribution systems (PDS), and educational facilities.

## 5. Public Facilities

- **Concept:** Facilities provided by the government to society as a collective unit (e.g., public health, sanitation, security, pollution-free environment, public transport).
    
- **Importance:** Money in your pocket cannot buy all the goods and services you need for a good quality of life (e.g., money cannot protect you from infectious diseases unless the whole community takes preventive steps).
    
- **Public Distribution System (PDS):** Ensures health and nutritional status of people in regions where ration shops function properly.
    

## 6. Human Development Index (HDI)

Published by the **United Nations Development Programme (UNDP)** in the _Human Development Report_, comparing countries based on:

1. **Health / Educational Standard:** Life expectancy at birth.
    
2. **Education:** Mean years of schooling and expected years of schooling.
    
3. **Economic Level:** Per Capita Gross National Income (GNI) in Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) dollars.
    

## 7. Sustainability of Development

- **Definition:** Development that meets the needs of the present generation without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
    
- **Issues of Overuse & Depletion:**
    
    - **Groundwater Overuse:** Heavy extraction for agriculture leads to declining water tables across regions like Punjab and Western UP.
        
    - **Exhaustion of Natural Resources:** Non-renewable resources (like crude oil) are finite and will run out if exploited at the current rate.
        
- **Path Forward:** Sustainable development requires renewable energy transitions, resource conservation, and eco-friendly technologies.
