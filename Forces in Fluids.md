### 1. Thrust and Pressure

- **Thrust:** The force acting on an object perpendicular (normal) to its surface is called thrust.
    
    - _SI Unit:_ Newton ($\text{N}$).
        
- **Pressure:** The thrust acting per unit area of a surface is called pressure.
    
    - **Formula:** $\text{Pressure} = \frac{\text{Thrust}}{\text{Area}}$
        
    - _SI Unit:_ $\text{N/m}^2$ or Pascal ($\text{Pa}$)
        
- **Everyday Examples of Pressure:**
    
    - **School bags with broad straps:** Wider straps increase the surface area, reducing the pressure on the student's shoulders.
        
    - **Sharp knives:** A sharp edge has a very small surface area, which exerts high pressure to cut objects easily.
        
    - **Camel's broad feet:** Broad feet increase the surface area, reducing pressure so the camel doesn't sink into loose sand.
        

### 2. Pressure in Fluids

- **Fluid:** Any substance that can flow is called a fluid. Both liquids and gases are fluids.
    
- **Key Characteristics:**
    
    - Fluids exert pressure in all directions on the walls of their containers.
        
    - Pressure increases with the depth of the fluid.
        

### 3. Buoyancy and Upthrust

- **Buoyancy:** The tendency of a fluid to exert an upward force on an object immersed partly or wholly in it is called buoyancy.
    
- **Buoyant Force (Upthrust):** The resultant upward force experienced by an object when placed in a fluid.
    
- **Factors Affecting Buoyant Force:**
    
    1. **Volume of the object:** Greater the volume of the object submerged, greater the upward thrust.
        
    2. **Density of the fluid:** Denser fluids exert a greater upward force (e.g., it is easier to swim in sea water than in fresh water because sea water has dissolved salts and higher density).
        

### 4. Archimedes' Principle

- **Statement:** When a body is partially or fully immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward force (buoyant force) that is equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it.
    
- **Apparent Weight Loss:**
    
    $$\text{Loss in weight of the object} = \text{Weight of fluid displaced} = \text{Buoyant force}$$
    
- **Conditions for Floating and Sinking:**
    
    - **Sink:** If the weight of the object is **greater** than the buoyant force ($W > F_B$), the object sinks.
        
    - **Float (Partially submerged):** If the weight of the object is **less** than or equal to the maximum buoyant force ($W \le F_B$), the object floats.
        
- **Applications:** Used in designing ships, submarines, lactometers (to check milk purity), and hydrometers.
    

### 5. Relative Density

- **Definition:** The relative density of a substance is the ratio of its density to that of water.
    
    - **Formula:** $\text{Relative Density} = \frac{\text{Density of the substance}}{\text{Density of water}}$
        
- Since it is a ratio of similar quantities, it has **no units**.
