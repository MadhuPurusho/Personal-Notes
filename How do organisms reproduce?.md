## 1. Introduction to Reproduction & DNA Copying

- **Reproduction:** The biological process by which living organisms give rise to new individuals of the same species, ensuring the continuity of life.
    
- **Role of DNA Copying:**
    
    - Chromosomes in the nucleus contain DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid), which carries information for inheriting traits from parents to offspring.
        
    - A basic event in reproduction is creating a DNA copy along with additional cellular apparatus.
        
    - **Variations:** DNA replication is not 100% accurate; slight inaccuracies lead to variations.
        

> [!info] Importance of Variation Variations help species adapt to changing environmental conditions (e.g., global warming, chemical changes) and increase their survival chances over time, driving evolution.

## 2. Modes of Asexual Reproduction

Asexual reproduction involves a **single parent** without the formation or fusion of gametes. Offspring are genetically identical clones of the parent.

|Mode|Process / Description|Examples|
|---|---|---|
|**Binary Fission**|Parent cell splits into two equal daughter cells in any plane or specific orientation.|_Amoeba_, _Paramecium_, _Leishmania_|
|**Multiple Fission**|Parent cell divides inside a protective cyst into many daughter cells simultaneously.|_Plasmodium_ (Malarial parasite)|
|**Fragmentation**|Body breaks into smaller fragments upon maturation; each fragment grows into a complete organism.|_Spirogyra_|
|**Regeneration**|Specialized cells proliferate to repair damaged tissues or reconstruct a complete organism from body cuts.|_Planaria_, _Hydra_|
|**Budding**|A outgrowth (bud) arises due to repeated cell division at a specific site, grows, and detaches when mature.|_Hydra_, Yeast|
|**Spore Formation**|Spores form inside protective structures called **sporangia**. Upon ripening, sporangia burst to release spores.|_Rhizopus_ (Bread mould)|

### 2.1 Vegetative Propagation in Plants

The process of growing new plants from vegetative parts (roots, stems, leaves) without seeds.

- **Natural Methods:**
    
    - **By Roots:** _Dahlia_, Sweet Potato
        
    - **By Stems:** Potato (tubers), Ginger (rhizome)
        
    - **By Leaves:** _Bryophyllum_ (buds along leaf margins)
        
- **Artificial Methods:** Layering, Grafting, Cutting.
    
- **Tissue Culture (Micropropagation):** Growing new plants from isolated plant cells/tissues in a sterile nutrient medium.
    

> [!note] Advantages of Vegetative Propagation
> 
> - Enables plants like Banana, Rose, Orange, and Jasmine to reproduce despite losing seed-producing capacity.
>     
> - Faster yield of flowers and fruits compared to plants grown from seeds.
>     
> - Maintains exact genetic traits of the parent plant.
>     

## 3. Sexual Reproduction

Involves **two parents** of opposite sexes producing specialized reproductive cells called **gametes** (germ cells) that fuse to form a single-celled **zygote**.

- **Why Sexual Reproduction?** Combines DNA from two different individuals, creating a higher degree of variations for evolutionary advantage.
    
- **Chromosome Number:** Gametes contain half the number of chromosomes (haploid) compared to body cells (diploid). Fusion restores the original chromosome number in the zygote.
    

## 4. Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

Flowering plants (**Angiosperms**) use flowers as their reproductive organs.

### Parts of a Flower

- **Stamen (Male Reproductive Part):** Consists of the Anther (produces pollen grains) and Filament.
    
- **Carpel / Pistil (Female Reproductive Part):** Consists of Stigma (sticky top to receive pollen), Style (elongated tube), and Ovary (swollen bottom containing ovules/eggs).
    

### 4.1 Types of Flowers

- **Unisexual Flowers:** Contain either stamen or carpel (e.g., Papaya, Watermelon).
    
- **Bisexual Flowers:** Contain both stamen and carpel (e.g., Hibiscus, Mustard).
    

### 4.2 Pollination & Fertilization

1. **Pollination:** Transfer of pollen grains from the **anther** to the **stigma**.
    
    - **Self-pollination:** Transfer within the same flower or plant.
        
    - **Cross-pollination:** Transfer between flowers of different plants of the same species (carried out by wind, water, or animals).
        
2. **Fertilization:**
    
    - Pollen germinates on the stigma, growing a **pollen tube** through the style to reach the ovary.
        
    - Male germ cell inside the pollen tube fuses with the female germ cell (egg) in the ovule to form a **zygote**.
        

### 4.3 Post-Fertilization Changes

- **Zygote** -> Embryo (future plant)
    
- **Ovule** -> Seed (develops a tough protective coat)
    
- **Ovary** -> Fruit (ripens and swells)
    
- **Petals, Sepals, Stamens** -> Wither and fall off
    

## 5. Human Reproductive System

### 5.1 Puberty & Changes

- **Puberty:** The phase of adolescence when sexual organs become functional and body maturity occurs.
    
- **Secondary Sexual Characteristics:**
    
    - **Common to both:** Hair growth in armpits and pubic regions, oily skin, acne.
        
    - **Boys:** Facial hair growth, deepening voice, enlargement of penis.
        
    - **Girls:** Breast enlargement, darkening of nipple skin, onset of menstruation.
        

### 5.2 Male Reproductive System

- **Testes:**
    
    - Located outside the abdominal cavity within the **Scrotum** to maintain a temperature 2–2.5°C lower than normal body temperature (required for sperm formation).
        
    - Functions: Production of male gametes (**sperms**) and secretion of the hormone **Testosterone**.
        
- **Vas Deferens:** Tube that transports sperms from testes towards the urethra.
    
- **Associated Glands (Prostate Gland & Seminal Vesicles):** Add secretions to nourish sperms and provide a fluid medium for fluid movement (**Semen**).
    
- **Urethra & Penis:** Urethra serves as a common passage for both urine and semen. Penis delivers semen into the female body.
    

### 5.3 Female Reproductive System

- **Ovaries:**
    
    - Pair of organs located in the lower abdomen.
        
    - Functions: Production of female gametes (**ova/eggs**) and secretion of hormones (**Estrogen** and **Progesterone**).
        
- **Oviducts / Fallopian Tubes:**
    
    - Receives the released egg from the ovary every month.
        
    - Site where **fertilization** occurs.
        
- **Uterus (Womb):**
    
    - An elastic, muscular bag where the embryo implants, receives nutrition, and develops during the **gestation period** (~9 months / 40 weeks).
        
    - **Placenta:** A specialized disc-like tissue embedded in the uterine wall providing oxygen and nutrients from the mother's blood to the embryo and removing waste.
        
- **Vagina:** Muscular canal acting as the birth canal.
    

### 5.4 What Happens When the Egg is Not Fertilized? (Menstruation)

- An ovary releases one egg every month while the uterus prepares its inner lining to receive a fertilized egg.
    
- If fertilization does not occur:
    
    1. The egg dies within 24 hours.
        
    2. The thick, spongy uterine lining breaks down along with its blood vessels.
        
    3. Blood and mucous flow out through the vagina as **Menstruation** (lasts 2–8 days per cycle).
        

## 6. Reproductive Health & Contraception

- **Reproductive Health:** Overall physical, mental, and social well-being in all aspects related to the reproductive system.
    
- **Sexually Transmitted Diseases (STDs):**
    
    - **Bacterial:** Gonorrhoea, Syphilis.
        
    - **Viral:** Warts, HIV-AIDS.
        

### 6.1 Contraceptive Methods

Methods used to prevent unwanted pregnancies and control population growth:

1. **Mechanical Barrier Methods:** Condoms, diaphragms, or cervical caps physically block sperms from reaching the egg. (Condoms also prevent STD transmission).
    
2. **Chemical Methods / Oral Contraceptives:** Birth control pills alter hormonal levels to prevent ovulation.
    
3. **Loop / Intrauterine Devices (IUDs):** Copper-T placed inside the uterus to prevent implantation.
    
4. **Surgical Methods:**
    
    - **Vasectomy:** Vas deferens in males is blocked/cut.
        
    - **Tubectomy:** Fallopian tubes in females are blocked/cut.
