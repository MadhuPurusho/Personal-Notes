### 1. Chapter Overview

**Author:** Ruskin Bond

**Genre:** Short Story / Moral Fiction

**Theme:** Trust, redemption, the power of education, and the transformative effect of human kindness.

> **Core Theme:** Unconditional trust and genuine kindness can reform a criminal far more effectively than suspicion, punishment, or law enforcement.

### 2. Character List

|**Character**|**Description / Role**|**Key Attributes**|
|---|---|---|
|**Hari Singh (The Narrator)**|A 15-year-old experienced thief who regularly changes his name to avoid the police.|Clever, manipulative, observant, yet possesses an active conscience.|
|**Anil**|A 25-year-old struggling freelance writer.|Simple, easygoing, trusting, generous, and forgiving.|

### 3. Detailed Plot Summary & Sequence of Events

#### 1. Meeting Anil

- The 15-year-old thief approaches Anil at a wrestling match.
    
- He introduces himself using the fake name **"Hari Singh"**. He frequently changes his name to stay ahead of his former employers and the police.
    
- Hari flatters Anil to gain his confidence, aiming to make Anil his next target.
    

#### 2. Staying with Anil & Learning

- Hari asks Anil for a job. Anil agrees to take him in and feed him on the condition that Hari knows how to cook.
    
- Hari lies about his cooking skills, and his first meal turns out so terrible that Anil feeds it to stray dogs.
    
- Instead of throwing Hari out, Anil laughs, teaches him to cook, and promises to teach him to **write his name, write full sentences, and add numbers**.
    
- Hari earns a small daily profit of about **one rupee** when buying daily supplies for the house. Anil notices this minor cheating but chooses to ignore it.
    

#### 3. The Theft

- Anil earns a living irregularly by writing for magazines. One evening, he returns home with a bundle of notes earned from selling a book to a publisher.
    
- Anil hides the money under his mattress.
    
- That night, while Anil is fast asleep, Hari slips his hand under the mattress and steals the money (₹600 in fifties). He slips out into the night.
    

#### 4. The Internal Conflict at the Station

- Hari runs to the railway station to catch the **10:30 Express to Lucknow**.
    
- As the train begins to move, something holds him back, and he hesitates to jump onto the carriage.
    
- He walks to the local bazaar while heavy rain sets in, soaking him and making the stolen currency notes damp.
    
- Sitting under the clock tower, Hari experiences a deep crisis of conscience:
    
    - Robbing a greedy or rich person is easy because they can afford the loss.
        
    - Robbing a trusting, careless person like Anil breaks his spirit, because Anil will feel sad not for the loss of money, but for the **loss of trust**.
        
    - He realizes that while stealing money offers short-term gain, learning to read and write will give him long-term dignity and unlimited potential as an educated man.
        

#### 5. Return and Forgiveness

- Driven by guilt and the desire to become educated, Hari returns to Anil's room and quietly slides the wet notes back under the mattress.
    
- The next morning, Anil offers Hari tea and hands him a damp ₹50 note, promising to pay him regularly from then on.
    
- Anil says, _"Today we'll start writing sentences"_.
    
- Though Anil clearly knows about the theft (the note was still wet), he says nothing to shame Hari or report him to the police. Hari smiles at Anil in his genuine, unforced way.
    

### 4. Key Literary Devices & Symbols

- **Symbolism:**
    
    - **The Stolen Money:** Represents quick, dishonest gratification and temptation.
        
    - **Education / Literacy:** Symbolizes long-term dignity, true wealth, and moral reform.
        
    - **The Rain:** Symbolizes the cleansing of Hari’s conscience and his moral renewal.
        
- **Irony:** Hari intended to rob Anil, but Anil ended up winning over Hari's moral character through pure trust.
    

### 5. Textual & Board-Style Questions

#### Q1. What does Hari Singh get from Anil in return for his work?

**Answer:** Initially, Anil offers Hari shelter and food in exchange for cooking. Over time, Anil teaches him to cook, write his name, write full sentences, and add numbers. Anil also turns a blind eye when Hari keeps a profit of one rupee a day from purchasing daily supplies.

#### Q2. Why did Hari Singh decide to return to Anil?

**Answer:** Hari realized that stealing money only provided temporary wealth, whereas the education Anil was offering could make him a respected, honest, and educated man. He felt immense guilt over breaking the unconditional trust of a kind man who treated him like a friend.

#### Q3. Did Anil know he had been robbed? How do you know?

**Answer:** Yes, Anil knew he had been robbed because the ₹50 note he gave Hari the next morning was still wet from the rain. However, he chose not to confront, shame, or report Hari, preferring to give him a second chance through kindness and forgiveness.

#### Q4. Why was it difficult for Hari Singh to rob Anil?

**Answer:** Hari found it difficult to rob Anil because Anil was completely trusting, careless, and kind-hearted. Hari felt that while robbing a greedy man is satisfying, robbing someone who trusts you blindly removes the "joy" of theft and deeply hurts the victim's faith in humanity.

### 6. Summary & Key Takeaways

- **Kindness Over Punishment:** Love, trust, and empathy can reform individuals where force or law fails.
    
- **Education as Empowerment:** True empowerment comes from knowledge and skills, which open doors to an honorable life.
