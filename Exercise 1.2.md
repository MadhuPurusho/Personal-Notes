### **Question 1: Prove that $\sqrt{5}$ is irrational.**

**Solution:**

Let us assume, on the contrary, that $\sqrt{5}$ is a rational number.

Therefore, we can find two co-prime integers $a$ and $b$ ($b \neq 0$) such that:

$$\sqrt{5} = \frac{a}{b}$$

_(Co-prime means $a$ and $b$ have no common factor other than $1$.)_

1. **Squaring both sides:**
    
    $$5 = \frac{a^2}{b^2}$$
    
    $$a^2 = 5b^2 \quad \text{--- (Equation 1)}$$
    
2. Since $5$ divides $a^2$, by Theorem 1.2, **$5$ must also divide $a$**.
    
3. So, we can express $a$ as $a = 5c$ for some integer $c$.
    
4. **Substituting $a = 5c$ into Equation 1:**
    
    $$(5c)^2 = 5b^2$$
    
    $$25c^2 = 5b^2$$
    
    $$b^2 = 5c^2$$
    
5. Since $5$ divides $b^2$, **$5$ must also divide $b$**.
    
6. From steps 2 and 5, **both $a$ and $b$ have $5$ as a common factor**.
    

**Conclusion:**

This contradicts our initial assumption that $a$ and $b$ are co-prime.

Therefore, our assumption that $\sqrt{5}$ is rational is false, and **$\sqrt{5}$ is irrational**.

### **Question 2: Prove that $3 + 2\sqrt{5}$ is irrational.**

**Solution:**

Let us assume, on the contrary, that $3 + 2\sqrt{5}$ is a rational number.

So, we can express it as:

$$3 + 2\sqrt{5} = \frac{a}{b}$$

where $a$ and $b$ are co-prime integers and $b \neq 0$.

1. **Rearranging the equation to isolate $\sqrt{5}$:**
    
    $$2\sqrt{5} = \frac{a}{b} - 3$$
    
    $$2\sqrt{5} = \frac{a - 3b}{b}$$
    
    $$\sqrt{5} = \frac{a - 3b}{2b}$$
    
2. Since $a$, $b$, $3$, and $2$ are integers, the term $\frac{a - 3b}{2b}$ is a rational number.
    
3. This implies that $\sqrt{5}$ must also be a rational number.
    
4. But this contradicts the proven fact that **$\sqrt{5}$ is an irrational number**.
    

**Conclusion:**

Our assumption that $3 + 2\sqrt{5}$ is rational is false.

Hence, **$3 + 2\sqrt{5}$ is irrational**.

### **Question 3: Prove that the following are irrationals:**

#### **(i) $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$**

**Solution:**

Let us assume that $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ is a rational number.

So, $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{a}{b}$, where $a$ and $b$ are co-prime integers and $b \neq 0$.

1. **Taking the reciprocal on both sides:**
    
    $$\sqrt{2} = \frac{b}{a}$$
    
2. Since $a$ and $b$ are integers, $\frac{b}{a}$ is a rational number.
    
3. This implies that $\sqrt{2}$ is rational, which contradicts the known fact that **$\sqrt{2}$ is irrational**.
    

**Conclusion:**

Therefore, **$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ is irrational**.

#### **(ii) $7\sqrt{5}$**

**Solution:**

Let us assume that $7\sqrt{5}$ is a rational number.

So, $7\sqrt{5} = \frac{a}{b}$, where $a$ and $b$ are co-prime integers and $b \neq 0$.

1. **Rearranging the terms:**
    
    $$\sqrt{5} = \frac{a}{7b}$$
    
2. Since $a$, $b$, and $7$ are integers, $\frac{a}{7b}$ is a rational number.
    
3. This implies that $\sqrt{5}$ is a rational number, which contradicts the fact that **$\sqrt{5}$ is irrational**.
    

**Conclusion:**

Therefore, **$7\sqrt{5}$ is irrational**.

#### **(iii) $6 + \sqrt{2}$**

3. This implies that $\sqrt{2}$ is rational, which contradicts the known fact that **$\sqrt{2}$ is irrational**.
    

**Conclusion:**

Therefore, **$6 + \sqrt{2}$ is irrational**.
