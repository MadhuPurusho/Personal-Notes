# Class 10 English Grammar 

## Overview of Tenses
> **Definition:** Tense indicates the time of an action or a state of being in relation to the present, past, or future. In Board examinations (CBSE / ICSE / State Boards), tenses are tested through **Gap Filling**, **Editing / Error Correction**, and **Sentence Transformation** (Reported Speech).

---

## 1. Present Tense

### Master Formula Table

| Tense Form | Sentence Structure / Formula | Example | Board Exam Keywords / Indicators |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple Present** | $S + V_1 (s/es) + O$ | She **writes** neatly. | Always, usually, daily, every day, universal truths |
| **Present Continuous** | $S + \text{is/am/are} + V_1 + \text{ing} + O$ | They **are playing** now. | Now, right now, at present, look!, listen! |
| **Present Perfect** | $S + \text{has/have} + V_3 + O$ | He **has finished** his work. | Already, just, yet, so far, recently, ever, never |
| **Present Perfect Continuous** | $S + \text{has/have} + \text{been} + V_1 + \text{ing} + \text{for/since} + O$ | I **have been studying** since morning. | Since + point of time, For + period of time |

### Crucial Usage Rules for Board Exams

#### A. Since vs. For
- **Since:** Used for a specific **point in time** (e.g., *since 2010, since 8 AM, since Monday, since childhood*).
- **For:** Used for a **duration or period of time** (e.g., *for 2 hours, for 5 years, for three days*).

#### B. Stative Verbs
Verbs related to perception, emotion, or possession are rarely used in continuous forms.
- **Incorrect:** *I am loving this movie.*
- **Correct:** *I love this movie.*
- **Common Stative Verbs:** know, believe, understand, hate, own, belong, seem, taste, smell.

## 2. Past Tense

### Master Formula Table

| Tense Form | Sentence Structure / Formula | Example | Board Exam Keywords / Indicators |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple Past** | $S + V_2 + O$ | She **visited** us yesterday. | Yesterday, ago, last night, in 1947, previous day |
| **Past Continuous** | $S + \text{was/were} + V_1 + \text{ing} + O$ | They **were watching** TV. | While, as, at that moment, when |
| **Past Perfect** | $S + \text{had} + V_3 + O$ | The train **had left** before I reached. | Before, after, by the time, already |
| **Past Perfect Continuous** | $S + \text{had} + \text{been} + V_1 + \text{ing} + \text{for/since} + O$ | She **had been working** for 3 hours. | For, since (in a past narrative context) |

### Crucial Usage Rules for Board Exams

#### A. The Dual Past Action Rule (Past Perfect vs. Simple Past)
When two actions happened in the past, the **first completed action** takes **Past Perfect ($S + \text{had} + V_3$)**, while the **later action** takes **Simple Past ($S + V_2$)**.

> **Golden Formula:** $\text{Action 1 (First)} \rightarrow \text{Past Perfect} \quad \Big| \quad \text{Action 2 (Second)} \rightarrow \text{Simple Past}$

- **Example:** *The patient **had died** (Action 1) before the doctor **arrived** (Action 2).*
- **Example:** *When I **reached** the station, the train **had already left**.*

#### B. Simultaneous Past Actions
When two actions were happening simultaneously in the past, both use **Past Continuous**.
- **Example:** *While my sister **was reading** a book, I **was playing** the piano.*

## 3. Future Tense

### Master Formula Table

| Tense Form | Sentence Structure / Formula | Example | Board Exam Keywords / Indicators |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple Future** | $S + \text{will/shall} + V_1 + O$ | I **will meet** him tomorrow. | Tomorrow, next week, soon, shortly |
| **Future Continuous** | $S + \text{will/shall} + \text{be} + V_1 + \text{ing} + O$ | She **will be studying** at 5 PM. | At this time tomorrow, at 6 PM next Monday |
| **Future Perfect** | $S + \text{will/shall} + \text{have} + V_3 + O$ | We **will have finished** by then. | By + future time expression (*by tomorrow, by 2030*) |
| **Future Perfect Continuous** | $S + \text{will/shall} + \text{have been} + V_1 + \text{ing} + O$ | He **will have been working** for 5 years. | By next month... for X years |

### Crucial Usage Rules for Board Exams

#### A. Conditionals in Future Tense
In a conditional sentence starting with **If / When / As soon as / Unless**, use **Simple Present** in the condition clause and **Simple Future** in the main result clause.
- **Incorrect:** *If you will study hard, you will pass.*
- **Correct:** *If you **study** hard, you **will pass**.*

## High-Yield Board Exam Practice Questions

### Type 1: Gap Filling (Choose / Fill the correct form)

1. **Question:** *By the time the police arrived, the burglar ________ (escape).*
   - **Answer:** **had escaped** *(Reason: Dual Past Action - burglary happened before police arrived)*.

2. **Question:** *My father ________ (read) the newspaper every morning.*
   - **Answer:** **reads** *(Reason: Habitual action requires Simple Present)*.

3. **Question:** *They ________ (live) in Delhi for ten years before moving to Mumbai.*
   - **Answer:** **had been living** *(Reason: Past Perfect Continuous for continuous past action prior to another past event)*.

---

### Type 2: Error Correction / Editing

1. **Incorrect:** *He is knowing the answer to this question.*
   - **Correct:** *He **knows** the answer to this question.* *(Reason: "Know" is a stative verb)*.

2. **Incorrect:** *She did not went to school yesterday.*
   - **Correct:** *She did not **go** to school yesterday.* *(Reason: "Did" is always followed by base verb $V_1$)*.

3. **Incorrect:** *I am living in this house since 2018.*
   - **Correct:** *I **have been living** in this house since 2018.* *(Reason: Action started in past and continues to present with "since")*.

---

## Summary / Quick Revision Sheet

- **Always check time markers:** *Ago/Yesterday* $\rightarrow V_2$ | *Since/For* $\rightarrow \text{has/have been } V_1+\text{ing}$ | *By tomorrow* $\rightarrow \text{will have } V_3$.
- **Auxiliary Rule:** After **do / does / did**, always use the **base verb ($V_1$)** without $s/es$ or past form.
- **Sequence of Tenses:** If the main verb of a narrative is in the past, subordinate clauses generally shift into past forms as well.
