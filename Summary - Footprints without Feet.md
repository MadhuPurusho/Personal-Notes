## Chapter 5: Footprints Without Feet
*By H.G. Wells*

> **Core Theme:** Misuse of scientific discoveries. The story illustrates how a brilliant scientific achievement can lead to total moral corruption, chaos, and self-destruction when used without ethics or responsibility.

## Detailed Plot Breakdown

### 1. Mystery of the Unseen Footprints
1. **The Discovery:** Two boys in London are startled to see fresh, muddy footprints appearing on a staircase with no visible person making them.
2. **The Cause:** The footprints belong to Griffin, an eccentric scientist who swallowed rare drugs to make his body as transparent as glass.
3. **The Escape:** Griffin had set fire to his landlord's house in revenge for being evicted. To escape unseen, he removed his clothes and became a homeless wanderer wandering barefoot in the freezing cold.

### 2. Adventures and Thefts in London
1. **The Big London Store:** To survive the winter cold, Griffin slips into a store. Once it closes, he wears warm clothes, shoes, and a hat, and feeds on cold meat, coffee, sweets, and wine.
2. **Narrow Escape:** In the morning, store assistants discover him. To avoid capture, Griffin strips off all his clothes and becomes invisible again.
3. **The Theatrical Company:** Griffin raids a shop in Drury Lane, wearing dark glasses, a fake nose, side-whiskers, and bandages around his head. He attacks the shopkeeper and steals his money.

### 3. The Strange Arrival at Iping
1. **Relocation:** Griffin takes a train to the quiet village of Iping and books two rooms at an inn run by Mrs. Hall.
2. **Suspicious Behavior:** His bandaged appearance and demand for solitude excite the curiosity of the villagers. Griffin falsely claims he was in an accident affecting his face.

### 4. Climax at the Village Inn
1. **The Burglary:** When Griffin runs out of money, he quietly steals cash from the local clergyman's study without being seen.
2. **Haunted Furniture:** The landlord and his wife find Griffin's room empty and inspect it. Suddenly, furniture begins moving on its own, and a chair charges at Mrs. Hall, driving her out of the room in panic.
3. **The Showdown:** When Mrs. Hall confronts Griffin, he loses his temper, removes his nose and bandages, revealing a headless body. Constable Jaffers arrives to arrest him, leading to a scuffle where Griffin strips off his clothes, turns fully invisible, knocks out Jaffers, and escapes into thin air.

---

## Key Themes & Symbolism

### 1. Science Without Morality
- Scientific advancements in the wrong hands cause havoc. Griffin's genius becomes a tool for revenge, theft, and assault rather than public good.

### 2. Isolation of the Outcast
- Although invisibility offers complete freedom, it deprives Griffin of human warmth, shelter, and companionship, turning him into a hunted fugitive.

## Important Practice Questions & Model Answers

### Q1: Why was Griffin considered a "lawless person"?
> **Answer:** Griffin used his scientific discoveries for criminal acts rather than constructive purposes. He burned down his landlord's house out of revenge, stole clothes, food, and money from London shops and the Iping clergyman, and physically assaulted innocent people like shopkeepers and police officers.

### Q2: How did Griffin manage to escape from the village of Iping?
1. He took advantage of the confusion when people saw him without a head.
2. He rapidly took off his garments piece by piece, becoming progressively less visible.
3. Once completely naked, he became invisible, landed punches on constable Jaffers and bystanders, and fled without being caught.

---

## Summary / Key Takeaways

- **Responsibility in Innovation:** Intelligence and scientific capability must be governed by ethics; otherwise, knowledge becomes a destructive weapon.
- **The Ultimate Fall:** Griffin's misuse of invisibility stripped him of his humanity, reducing him to a naked, shivering wanderer hiding from society.
