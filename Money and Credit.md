## 1. Money as a Medium of Exchange

### Barter System & Double Coincidence of Wants

- **Barter System:** The direct exchange of goods and services for other goods and services without using money as a intermediate.
    
- **Double Coincidence of Wants:** A fundamental requirement of the barter system where both parties must agree to sell and buy each other's commodities.
    
- **Elimination of the Problem:** Money acts as an intermediate in the exchange process, eliminating the need for a double coincidence of wants.
    

## 2. Modern Forms of Money

### Currency

- Includes paper notes and metallic coins.
    
- **Authorization:** Modern currency holds value because it is authorized by the country's government.
    
- **Issuing Authority in India:** The **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** issues currency notes on behalf of the central government. By law, no other individual or organization can issue currency.
    
- The Rupee ($\text{₹}$) is widely accepted in India as a legal tender that cannot be legally refused in settling transactions.
    

### Deposits with Banks

- **Demand Deposits:** Money deposited in bank accounts that can be withdrawn on demand.
    
- Banks pay interest on these deposits.
    
- **Cheques:** A paper instructing a bank to pay a specific amount from a person's account to the person named on the cheque. It allows direct settlement of payments without using cash.
    

## 3. [[Loan Activities of Banks]]

- **Cash Reserve:** Banks in India keep around **15%** of their total deposits as cash to handle daily withdrawals by depositors.
    
- **Lending:** The major portion of deposits is used to extend loans for economic activities.
    
- **Main Source of Bank Income:** The difference between the higher interest charged on loans and the lower interest paid on deposits.
    

## 4. Two Different Credit Situations

**Credit (Loan)** refers to an agreement where the lender provides money, goods, or services to the borrower with a promise of future repayment.

1. **Positive Outcome (Role in Growth):**
    
    - _Example:_ A manufacturer takes a loan for festive production, delivers on time, earns a profit, and repays the loan.
        
    - _Effect:_ Income increases and the borrower is better off.
        
2. **Negative Outcome (Debt Trap):**
    
    - _Example:_ A farmer takes a loan for crop cultivation, but crops fail due to pest attack or drought.
        
    - _Effect:_ The farmer is forced to sell land or take another loan to repay the original debt. This pushes the borrower into a **debt trap**.
        

## 5. [[Terms of Credit]]

The conditions under which a loan is granted are known as the **Terms of Credit**:

1. **Interest Rate:** The explicit percentage charged on the principal amount.
    
2. **Collateral:** An asset owned by the borrower (e.g., land, vehicle, livestock, bank deposits) used as security/guarantee to the lender until the loan is paid off. If the borrower defaults, the lender has the right to sell it.
    
3. **Documentation Required:** Papers showing income, identity, employment, or property ownership.
    
4. **Mode of Repayment:** Duration and method (e.g., monthly installments, lump sum).
    

## 6. [[Formal and Informal Sectors of Credit in India]]

|**Feature**|**Formal Sector**|**Informal Sector**|
|---|---|---|
|**Sources**|Banks and Cooperatives.|Moneylenders, traders, employers, friends, relatives.|
|**Supervising Body**|**Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** monitors their functions.|No official government body to supervise operations.|
|**Interest Rates**|Generally low and reasonable.|Very high and exploitative.|
|**Collateral Requirement**|Strictly requires collateral and proper documentation.|Often granted without formal collateral.|
|**Primary Beneficiaries**|Urban and relatively wealthier households.|Rural poor households lacking documentation.|

### Why cheap and affordable credit is necessary:

- It enables small farmers, businesses, and artisans to invest and expand.
    
- It prevents people from falling into exploitative debt traps.
    
- It drives national economic growth and development.
    

## 7. Self-Help Groups (SHGs) for the Poor

- **Composition:** Typically 15–20 members (mostly women in rural neighborhoods) who meet and save regularly.
    
- **Functioning:**
    
    - Members can take small loans from the group's pooled savings at low interest rates.
        
    - If the group is regular in savings for 1–2 years, it becomes eligible to get a formal loan directly from a bank **without individual collateral**.
        
- **Importance:**
    
    - Helps rural poor overcome the barrier of lack of collateral and paperwork.
        
    - Empowers women socially and economically.
        
    - Provides a platform to discuss social issues such as health, nutrition, and domestic violence.
