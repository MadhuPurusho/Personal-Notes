**Majoritarianism:** A belief that the majority community should be able to rule a country in whichever way it wants, completely disregarding the wishes and needs of the minority.

### Measures Taken to Establish Sinhala Supremacy

After independence in 1948, the democratically elected government adopted majoritarian policies:

1. **Act of 1956:** Sinhala was declared the only official language of Sri Lanka, disregarding Tamil.
    
2. **Preferential Policies:** The government favored Sinhala applicants for university positions and government jobs.
    
3. **State Protection of Religion:** A new constitution stipulated that the state should foster and protect Buddhism.
    

### Consequences for Sri Lanka

- **Alienation:** Sri Lankan Tamils felt that none of the major political parties led by Buddhist Sinhalas were sensitive to their language and culture.
    
- **Struggles & Demands:** Tamils launched political parties demanding:
    
    - Official recognition of the Tamil language.
        
    - Regional autonomy.
        
    - Equality of opportunity in education and jobs.
        
    - An independent state named **Tamil Eelam** in northern and eastern parts of Sri Lanka.
        
- **Civil War:** Widespread distrust escalated into a civil war. Thousands of people from both communities were killed, families became refugees, and the country suffered severe social and economic damage until the war ended in 2009.
