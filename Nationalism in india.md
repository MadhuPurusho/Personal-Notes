## 1. First World War, Khilafat, and Non-Cooperation (Pages 29–33)

### Economic and Political Effects of WWI (1914–1918)

- **Increase in Defense Expenditure:** Funded by war loans, increased taxes, custom duties, and the introduction of income tax.
    
- **Price Hike:** Prices doubled between 1913 and 1918, causing extreme hardship for common people.
    
- **Forced Recruitment:** Villagers were forcibly recruited into the British army, leading to widespread anger.
    
- **Crop Failures & Famine:** Crop failures across India (1918–1919 and 1920–1921) led to acute food shortages, compounded by an influenza epidemic that killed millions.
    

### The Idea of Satyagraha

- **Concept:** Emphasized the power of truth and the need to search for truth without physical force, violence, or aggression.
    
- **Mahatma Gandhi's Return:** Returned to India from South Africa in **January 1915**.
    
- **Early Local Satyagraha Movements:**
    
    1. **Champaran, Bihar (1917):** Inspired peasants against the oppressive indigo plantation system.
        
    2. **Kheda, Gujarat (1917/1918):** Supported peasants demanding relaxation of land revenue payment due to crop failure.
        
    3. **Ahmedabad, Gujarat (1918):** Organized satyagraha for cotton mill workers demanding higher wages.
        

### The Rowlatt Act (1919) & Jallianwala Bagh

- **The Rowlatt Act:** Passed hastily by the Imperial Legislative Council despite Indian opposition.
    
    - Gave enormous power to the government to repress political activities.
        
    - Allowed detention of political prisoners without trial for up to 2 years.
        
- **Rowlatt Satyagraha:** Gandhi called for a nationwide _hartal_ (strike) on **April 6, 1919**. Rallies were organized, and workers went on strike.
    
- **Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (April 13, 1919):**
    
    - A large crowd gathered at Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar to attend the Baisakhi fair and protest the arrest of leaders.
        
    - **General Dyer** blocked exit points and ordered open fire on the unarmed crowd, killing hundreds.
        
    - _Outcome:_ Protests and clashes broke out; Gandhi withdrew the movement due to rising violence.
        

### The Khilafat Movement & Need for Non-Cooperation

- **Khilafat Issue:** Harsh terms were set to be imposed on the Ottoman Emperor (the spiritual head/Khalifa of the Islamic world) after WWI.
    
- **Khilafat Committee:** Formed in Bombay (March 1919) by **Muhammad Ali** and **Shaukat Ali**.
    
- **Unity with Congress:** Gandhi saw this as an opportunity to unite Hindus and Muslims under one movement.
    
- **Calcutta Session (Sept 1920):** Gandhi convinced Congress leaders to adopt Non-Cooperation for Swaraj and Khilafat.
    
- **Nagpur Session (Dec 1920):** The Non-Cooperation programme was formally adopted by the Congress.
    

## 2. Differing Strands Within the Movement (Pages 34–39)

The Non-Cooperation-Khilafat Movement began in **January 1921**.

```
                Differing Strands of Non-Cooperation Movement
                                      |
         +----------------------------+----------------------------+
         |                            |                            |
  1. Towns/Cities             2. Countryside               3. Plantations
  • Middle class participation • Peasant revolts (Awadh)    • Assam workers
  • Boycott of schools/goods  • Tribal revolts (Gudem)     • Demand to move freely
```

### A. The Movement in Towns

- **Action:** Students left government schools, teachers resigned, and lawyers gave up legal practice. Council elections were boycotted (except in Madras by the Justice Party).
    
- **Economic Impact:** Foreign goods boycotted, foreign cloth burned in bonfires, liquor shops picketed. Foreign cloth import halved between 1921 and 1922.
    
- **Why it Slowed Down:**
    
    - **Khadi** was too expensive compared to mass-produced mill cloth.
        
    - Alternative Indian national institutions were slow to develop, forcing students and lawyers to return to British institutions.
        

### B. Rebellion in the Countryside

#### Peasant Movement in Awadh

- **Leader:** **Baba Ramchandra** (a sanyasi who had been an indentured laborer in Fiji).
    
- **Grievances:** Extremely high rents, oppressive demands for _begar_ (unpaid forced labor), and lack of tenure security from _talukdars_ and landlords.
    
- **Actions:** Demanded reduction of revenue, abolition of _begar_, and social boycott of oppressive landlords (_nai-dhobi bandhs_).
    
- **Oudh Kisan Sabha:** Formed in October 1920, headed by Jawaharlal Nehru and Baba Ramchandra.
    

#### Tribal Revolt in Gudem Hills (Andhra Pradesh)

- **Leader:** **Alluri Sitaram Raju** (claimed to have special powers, inspired by Gandhi, but believed force was necessary).
    
- **Grievances:** The colonial government closed forest areas, preventing tribals from grazing cattle, gathering fuel, or practicing shifting cultivation.
    
- **Actions:** Tribals engaged in guerrilla warfare, attacking police stations and attempting to kill British officials. Raju was captured and executed in 1924.
    

### C. Swaraj in the Plantations

- **Context:** Plantation workers in Assam were restricted under the **Inland Emigration Act of 1859**, which barred them from leaving tea gardens without permission.
    
- **Reaction:** Hearing of Non-Cooperation, thousands defied authorities, left plantations, and headed home, believing "Gandhi Raj" was coming.
    
- **Outcome:** Stranded due to railway and steamer strikes, they were caught by police and brutally beaten.
    

### D. Withdrawal of Non-Cooperation Movement

- **Chauri Chaura Incident (February 1922):** A peaceful demonstration at Chauri Chaura (Gorakhpur, UP) turned violent when a crowd set fire to a police station, killing 22 policemen.
    
- **Withdrawal:** Realizing the movement was turning violent and satyagrahis needed proper training, Mahatma Gandhi called off the Non-Cooperation Movement.
