## 1. Chapter Overview: A Letter to God

**Author:** Gregorio López y Fuentes  
**Genre:** Short Story / Satire  
**Theme:** Unshakable faith in God, humanity, ironical nature of human actions, and innocence.

> **Core Theme:** The story highlights the immense, unyielding faith of a simple farmer in God, while contrasting it with the ironic nature of human kindness and perception.

| Character | Role / Description | Key Traits |

## 3. Detailed Plot Summary & Sequence of Events

### Step-by-Step Narrative Breakdown

1. **The Hope for Rain:**
   * Lencho lives with his family on the peak of a low hill, overlooking his cornfield.
   * His field requires a good downpour or shower to yield a rich harvest.
   * Observing the sky in the northeast, Lencho predicts rain, which fills his family with joy.

2. **The Destruction by Hailstorm:**
   * Rain begins as predicted during dinner, and Lencho compares the raindrops to coins (big drops as 10-cent pieces and small ones as 5-cent pieces).
   * Soon, strong winds blow, bringing a devastating **hailstorm** that lasts for an hour.
   * The field turns completely white, as if covered in salt. The entire corn crop is destroyed, leaving no leaves on the trees.

3. **Lencho's Supreme Faith & The Letter:**
   * Facing starvation and total ruin, Lencho holds onto a single hope: **help from God**.
   * On Sunday morning, he writes a letter addressed directly to God requesting **100 pesos** to replant his field and survive until the next harvest.
   * He drops the letter into the mailbox at the town post office.

4. **The Postmaster's Gesture of Kindness:**
   * A postman finds the uniquely addressed letter and shows it to the postmaster, who initially laughs.
   * Realizing the depth of Lencho's faith, the postmaster decides to respond to the letter so Lencho's belief is not shaken.
   * He gathers money from his employees, contributes a part of his salary, and asks friends for donations. Together, they manage to collect **70 pesos**.
   * He places the money in an envelope signed simply as **"God"**.

5. **The Irony of the Outcome:**
   * Lencho returns the following Sunday to collect his reply.
   * Upon opening the envelope, he shows no surprise at seeing the money, demonstrating his absolute faith.
   * However, upon counting 70 pesos instead of 100, he becomes angry, believing God could not have made a mistake.
   * Lencho writes a second letter immediately, asking God to send the remaining 30 pesos—but **not through the mail**, calling the post office employees a **"bunch of crooks."**

---

## 4. Key Literary Devices & Symbols

### Literary Elements

* **Irony (Situational):** The central element of the story. The post office workers collect money out of compassion to keep Lencho's faith alive, yet Lencho suspects them of stealing the missing 30 pesos and calls them a "bunch of crooks."
* **Metaphor:**
  * Raindrops compared to **"new coins."**
  * Hailstones compared to **"frozen pearls."**
  * The destruction compared to a **"plague of locusts."**
  * Lencho described as an **"ox of a man"** (highlighting his hard-working nature).

---

## 5. Summary & Key Takeaways

* **Unquestioning Faith:** Lencho's faith is so pure that he expects a direct reply and money from God without a trace of doubt.
* **Human Kindness:** The postmaster exemplifies selfless altruism, helping a stranger solely to protect an innocent belief.
* **The Irony of Perception:** Human bias can cause people to mistake genuine acts of goodwill for deception when expectations are not fully met.
