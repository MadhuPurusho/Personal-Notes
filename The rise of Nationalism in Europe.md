### 1. The French Revolution & the Idea of the Nation

- **First Expression of Nationalism:** Began with the French Revolution in 1789.
    
- **Creating Collective Identity:**
    
    - Ideas of **La Patrie** (the fatherland) and **Le Citoyen** (the citizen) emphasized a united community.
        
    - A new tricolor French flag replaced the royal standard.
        
    - Formation of the National Assembly elected by active citizens.
        
    - French became the common national language.
        
- **Napoleonic Code (Civil Code of 1804):**
    
    - Established equality before the law and secured property rights.
        
    - Abolished the feudal system and freed peasants from serfdom.
        
    - Removed guild restrictions and improved transport/communication.
        
    - Drawbacks: Increased taxation, strict censorship, and forced conscription into the army.
        

### 2. The Making of Nationalism in Europe

#### Social Structure (Mid-18th Century)

- **Aristocracy:** Dominant, wealthy, land-owning class that spoke French.
    
- **Peasantry:** Majority of the population working on rented or feudal land.
    
- **New Middle Class:** Emerged due to industrialization; composed of businessmen, industrialists, and professionals who advocated for national unity.
    

#### Liberal Nationalism

- **Political Sphere:** Stood for individual freedom, equality before the law, representative government, and end of clerical privileges.
    
- **Economic Sphere:** Stood for freedom of markets and removal of state-imposed trade restrictions.
    
- **[[Zollverein]] (1834):** A customs union formed by Prussia that abolished tariff barriers and reduced currencies from over 30 to just two.
    

#### New Conservatism after 1815

- European monarchies defeated Napoleon in 1815.
    
- **Treaty of Vienna (1815):** Hosted by Austrian Chancellor [[Duke Metternich]] to restore monarchies.
    
    - Bourbon dynasty restored in France.
        
    - France lost conquered territories.
        
    - States created around France to block future expansion.
        

#### The Revolutionaries

- Underground secret societies formed to oppose monarchies.
    
- **[[Giuseppe Mazzini]]:** Italian revolutionary who founded **Young Italy** (Marseilles) and **Young Europe** (Berne) to promote a unified Italian republic.
    

### 3. The Age of Revolutions (1830–1848)

- **July Revolution (1830):** Bourbon kings overthrown in France; constitutional monarchy set up under Louis Philippe.
    
- **Greek War of Independence:** Struggle against the Ottoman Empire; Treaty of Constantinople (1832) recognized Greece as an independent nation.
    
- **[[Romanticism]]:** Cultural movement emphasizing art, poetry, folk culture, and emotion to develop national feeling.
    
- **Hunger & Hardship (1830s):** Massive population growth, food shortage, and unemployment led to widespread peasant revolts across Europe.
    
- **1848 Liberal Revolution:** Middle class demanded parliamentary principles, a constitution, and national unity.

### 5. Visualising the Nation & Allegories

- Artists used female allegories to represent nations visually:
    
    - **Marianne (France):** Symbolized liberty and the republic (red cap, tricolor, cockade).
        
    - **Germania (Germany):** Wore an oak crown representing heroism.
        

### 6. Nationalism and Imperialism

- By the late 19th century, nationalism transformed into narrow rivalry among European powers.
    
- **The Balkans:** A region of intense ethnic conflict under Ottoman decay. European powers exploited these conflicts to expand influence, ultimately triggering **World War I** in 1914.
