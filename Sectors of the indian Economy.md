## 1. Classification Based on the Nature of Economic Activity

Economic activities are categorized into three major sectors based on how goods and services are produced:

### A. Primary Sector (Agriculture and Related Sector)

- **Definition:** Activities where goods are produced by directly exploiting or extracting natural resources.
    
- **Examples:** Farming, dairy, fishing, forestry, and mining.
    
- **Why it matters:** It forms the primary base for all other products that are subsequently manufactured.
    

### B. Secondary Sector (Industrial Sector)

- **Definition:** Activities in which natural raw materials from the primary sector are transformed into processed goods through manufacturing or construction.
    
- **Examples:** Spinning cotton fiber into cloth, converting sugarcane into sugar, or constructing buildings and roads.
    
- **Why it matters:** It adds value to raw materials and builds industrial infrastructure.
    

### C. Tertiary Sector (Service Sector)

- **Definition:** Activities that do not directly produce physical goods, but provide aid, support, and services to assist the primary and secondary sectors.
    
- **Examples:** Transport, communication, banking, trade, healthcare, and education.
    
- **Why it matters:** It generates value and supports overall economic functioning.
    

## 2. [[Gross Domestic Product (GDP) & Interdependence]]

- **Interdependence:** The three sectors rely heavily on one another. For instance, agriculture needs industrial tools and transport services, while industries need raw materials and logistical support.
    
- **Gross Domestic Product (GDP):** The total monetary value of all **final goods and services** produced within a country during a specific year.
    
- **Counting Final Goods Only:** Intermediate goods (like flour used to bake bread) are excluded to prevent double-counting their value in GDP calculations.
    

## 3. Rising Importance of the Tertiary Sector in India

Over the past few decades, the tertiary sector has emerged as the largest producing sector in India. Reasons include:

1. **Demand for Basic Services:** Increased need for hospitals, educational institutions, defense, courts, and administrative offices managed or supported by the government.
    
2. **Growth of Primary & Secondary Sectors:** As agriculture and manufacturing grow, the demand for transport, trade, and storage increases proportionally.
    
3. **Rising Income Levels:** Higher income levels drive demand for lifestyle services like tourism, eating out, private schooling, and shopping.
    
4. **Information & Technology:** Rapid development in ICT (Information and Communication Technology) and global software services.
    

## 4. Unemployment & Division of Labor

- **Disguised Unemployment:** A situation where more people are working on a task than are actually needed. Even if a few workers are removed, total production remains unchanged (common in rural agriculture).
    
- **MGNREGA 2005 (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act):**
    
    - Guarantees **100 days of wage employment** per year to rural households in need.
        
    - If the government fails to provide employment within 15 days, an unemployment allowance is provided.
        

## 5. Classification Based on Working Conditions

|**Parameter**|**Organised Sector**|**Unorganised Sector**|
|---|---|---|
|**Registration & Laws**|Registered with the government; follows formal labor laws.|Outside government control; laws are largely ignored or not applicable.|
|**Job Security**|High job security and fixed working hours.|Low job security; workers can be asked to leave anytime.|
|**Benefits**|Paid leaves, medical benefits, provident fund, and pension.|No paid leaves, medical security, or formal retirement benefits.|
|**Examples**|Government employees, registered corporate workers.|Daily wage laborers, small shop assistants, street vendors.|

## 6. Classification Based on Ownership

- **Public Sector:**
    
    - Owned and controlled by the government.
        
    - **Primary Goal:** Public welfare and development rather than profit maximization.
        
    - _Examples:_ Indian Railways, Post Offices.
        
- **Private Sector:**
    
    - Owned and managed by private individuals or companies.
        
    - **Primary Goal:** Profit generation.
        
    - _Examples:_ TISCO, Reliance Industries, Infosys.
