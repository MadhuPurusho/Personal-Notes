Belgian leaders took a path opposite to Sri Lanka's by recognizing regional and cultural differences. Between 1970 and 1993, they amended their constitution **four times** to create an innovative governance model.

### Key Elements of the Belgian Model

- **Equal Representation in Central Government:** The constitution prescribes that the number of Dutch and French-speaking ministers shall be equal in the central government. Laws require majority approval from both linguistic groups.
    
- **Devolution of Power:** State governments in the two regions were granted significant authority and are not subordinate to the central government.
    
- **Equal Power in Brussels:** The capital city, Brussels, has a separate government where both Dutch and French communities have equal representation.
    
- **Community Government:** A third tier of government elected by people belonging to one language community (Dutch, French, or German), regardless of where they live. It holds power regarding cultural, educational, and language-related issues.
    

> **Key Outcome:** Belgium successfully avoided civic strife and partition along linguistic lines. Recognizing its stability, the **European Union (EU)** chose Brussels as its headquarters.
