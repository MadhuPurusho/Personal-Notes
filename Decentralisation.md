# Decentralisation

When power is taken away from Central and State governments and given to Local government.

### Rationale Behind Decentralisation

- Many problems and issues are best settled at the local level.
- Local people have better knowledge of local problems, resource allocation, and priority management.
- Enables direct democratic participation.

### The Constitutional Amendment of 1992 (73rd & 74th Amendments)

Before 1992, local bodies were directly under state government control, lacked regular elections, and held no real power. The 1992 amendment made local governance effective:

- **Mandatory Elections:** Regular elections to local government bodies are constitutionally required.
- **Reserved Seats:** Seats are reserved for Scheduled Castes (SC), Scheduled Tribes (ST), and Other Backward Classes (OBC).
- **Reservation for Women:** At least one-third (33%) of all positions are reserved for women.
- **State Election Commission:** An independent body created in each state to conduct panchayat and municipal elections.
- **Revenue Sharing:** State governments must share powers and revenue with local bodies.

### Rural Local Government (Panchayati Raj)

- **Gram Panchayat:** Council consisting of ward members (Panch) and a head (Sarpanch) at the village level. Elected directly by adult residents.
- **Gram Sabha:** General body consisting of all voters in the village. Meets 2 to 3 times a year to approve the Gram Panchayat budget and review its work.
- **Panchayat Samiti / Block / Mandal:** Formed by grouping several Gram Panchayats together. Members are elected by spatial representatives.
- **Zilla Parishad:** Top tier covering the entire district. Formed by all Panchayat Samitis in a district. Led by a political head (Zilla Parishad Chairperson). MPs and MLAs of the district are also members.

### Urban Local Government

- **Municipalities:** Set up in smaller towns, headed by a Municipal Chairperson.
- **Municipal Corporations:** Set up in large cities, headed by an elected official called a Mayor.
