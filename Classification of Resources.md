## Meaning of Resources

Anything in the environment that can be used to satisfy human needs, has utility, and is technologically accessible, economically feasible, and culturally acceptable is called a resource.

Resources can be classified in different ways.

## 1. Classification Based on Origin

### Biotic Resources

- Obtained from the biosphere.
- Have life or are derived from living organisms.
- Examples:
  - Plants
  - Animals
  - Forests
  - Fisheries
  - Human beings

### Abiotic Resources

- Obtained from non-living things.
- Examples:
  - Rocks
  - Metals
  - Water
  - Soil
  - Minerals

---

## 2. Classification Based on Exhaustibility

### Renewable Resources

- Can be renewed or reproduced naturally.
- Examples:
  - Solar energy
  - Wind energy
  - Water
  - Forests
  - Wildlife

> Renewable resources can become depleted if people use them faster than nature can replace them.

### Non-Renewable Resources

- Exist in limited quantities.
- Take a very long time to form.
- Once exhausted, they cannot be replaced quickly.
- Examples:
  - Coal
  - Petroleum
  - Natural gas
  - Minerals

---

## 3. Classification Based on Ownership

### Individual Resources

- Owned privately by individuals.
- Examples:
  - Houses
  - Cars
  - Private wells
  - Agricultural land

### Community-Owned Resources

- Resources accessible to members of a community.
- Examples:
  - Public parks
  - Village ponds
  - Grazing grounds
  - Public playgrounds

### National Resources

- Resources belonging to the nation.
- The government has the authority to use and manage them.
- Examples:
  - Roads
  - Railways
  - Rivers
  - Forests
  - Minerals
  - Resources within territorial waters

### International Resources

- Resources that lie beyond national boundaries and are regulated through international institutions.
- No individual country can use these resources without following international agreements.

---

## 4. Classification Based on the Status of Development

### Potential Resources

- Resources found in a region that have not been fully utilised.
- Example:
  - Rajasthan and Gujarat have potential for solar and wind energy.

### Developed Resources

- Resources whose quantity and quality have been determined.
- They are being used for current development.

### Stock

- Resources that have the potential to satisfy human needs but lack the required technology to use them.
- Example:
  - Hydrogen can be used as a source of energy, but suitable technology may limit its large-scale use.

### Reserves

- Part of the stock that can be used to satisfy future needs with existing technology.
- Example:
  - Water stored in dams can be used for future electricity generation and irrigation.

---

# Quick Revision

| Basis | Types |
|---|---|
| Origin | Biotic, Abiotic |
| Exhaustibility | Renewable, Non-renewable |
| Ownership | Individual, Community, National, International |
| Status of Development | Potential, Developed, Stock, Reserves |

## Important Difference

Renewable → Can be renewed naturally.

Non-renewable → Limited and takes a very long time to form.

Potential → Available but not fully utilised.

Developed → Surveyed, measured and currently being used.

Stock → Can satisfy needs, but technology is not yet sufficient.

Reserves → Part of stock that can be used with current technology.
