## 1. Biodiversity and Flora & Fauna in India

- **Biodiversity (Biological Diversity):** The rich variety of living organisms (plants, animals, microorganisms) existing in a system, integrated through interdependent networks.
    
- **Flora & Fauna Status:** India is one of the world's richest countries in biological diversity, harboring about 8% of the total number of species in the world (~1.6 million).
    
- **Threatened Species:** At least 10% of India's recorded wild flora and 20% of its mammals are on the threatened list (e.g., cheetah, pink-headed duck, mountain quail, forest spotted owlet, and plants like _Madhuca insignis_ and _Hubbardia heptaneuron_).
    

## 2. IUCN Classification of Species

The International Union for Conservation of Nature and Natural Resources (IUCN) categorizes plant and animal species as follows:

|**Category**|**Definition**|**Examples**|
|---|---|---|
|**Normal Species**|Population levels are considered normal for survival.|Cattle, sal, pine, rodents.|
|**Endangered Species**|In danger of extinction if negative factors continue to operate.|Blackbuck, crocodile, Indian wild ass, Indian rhino, lion-tailed macaque, sangai.|
|**Vulnerable Species**|Population has declined to levels where it is likely to move into endangered category soon.|Blue sheep, Asiatic elephant, Gangetic dolphin.|
|**Rare Species**|Species with small populations that may move into endangered/vulnerable if negative factors persist.|Himalayan brown bear, wild Asiatic buffalo, desert fox, hornbill.|
|**Endemic Species**|Found only in particular areas isolated by natural or geographical barriers.|Andaman teal, Nicobar pigeon, Andaman wild pig, mithun in Arunachal Pradesh.|
|**Extinct Species**|Not found after searches in known or likely areas where they occurred.|Asiatic cheetah, pink-headed duck.|

## 3. Causes of Depletion of Flora and Fauna

1. **Colonial Expansion:** Railway expansion, agriculture, commercial and scientific forestry, and mining operations during the colonial period.
    
2. **Agricultural Expansion:** Over 26,000 sq km of forest area was converted into agricultural land across India between 1951 and 1980.
    
3. **Large-Scale River Valley Projects:** Submerged large tracts of forests (e.g., clearing over 5,000 sq km for the Narmada Sagar Project in Madhya Pradesh).
    
4. **Mining:** Major cause of deforestation (e.g., dolomite mining threatening the Buxa Tiger Reserve in West Bengal).
    
5. **Overgrazing and Fuelwood Collection:** Continuous pressure on forest resources for domestic energy and fodder needs.
    
6. **Poaching and Habitat Destruction:** Hunting, illegal trade of animal parts, forest fires, and environmental pollution.
    

## 4. Conservation of Forest and Wildlife in India

Conservation preserves ecological diversity and life support systems (water, air, soil) while maintaining the genetic diversity of plants and animals.

### Key Conservation Steps:

- **The Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972:**
    
    - Implemented to protect habitats with an all-India list of protected species.
        
    - Banned hunting, gave legal protection to habitats, and restricted trade in wildlife.
        
    - Established **National Parks** and **Wildlife Sanctuaries**.
        
- **Specific Wildlife Projects:** Focused on saving endangered species:
    
    - **Project Tiger (1973):** One of the well-publicized wildlife initiatives to protect tiger populations. Famous reserves include **Corbett** (Uttarakhand), **Sunderbans** (West Bengal), **Bandhavgarh** (Madhya Pradesh), and **Sariska** (Rajasthan).
        
    - Other projects target the one-horned rhinoceros, Kashmir stag (_hangul_), three types of crocodiles, and the Asiatic lion.
        

## 5. Types and Distribution of Forest Resources

Forests in India are classified into three legal categories by the government:

### A. Reserved Forests

- Regarded as the **most valuable** for the conservation of forest and wildlife resources.
    
- More than **half** of total forest land in India is declared as reserved.
    
- Strictly controlled by government; local activities restricted.
    

### B. Protected Forests

- Almost **one-third** of total forest area is protected.
    
- Protected from any further depletion; limited rights given to local communities.
    

> **Note:** Reserved and Protected forests are together known as **Permanent Forest Estates**, maintained for producing timber and other forest produce. _Madhya Pradesh_ has the largest area under permanent forests (75%).

### C. Unclassed Forests

- Consist of forests and wastelands belonging to both government and private individuals/communities.
    
- Mainly found in **North-Eastern states** and parts of **Gujarat**, where a high percentage of forests are managed by local communities.
    

## 6. Community and Conservation

Local communities in India have played a significant role in conserving forests:

- **Sariska Tiger Reserve (Rajasthan):** Villagers fought against mining by citing the Wildlife Protection Act.
    
- **Bhairodev Dakav 'Sonchuri' (Alwar, Rajasthan):** Inhabitants of five villages declared 1,200 hectares of forest as a sanctuary, creating their own rules to prohibit hunting and protect wildlife.
    
- **Chipko Movement (Himalayas):** Led by villagers to successfully resist deforestation and promote indigenous afforestation.
    
- **Beej Bachao Andolan (Tehri) & Navdanya:** Showed that diversified crop production without synthetic chemicals is economically viable.
    
- **Joint Forest Management (JFM):**
    
    - Involves local communities in the management and restoration of degraded forests.
        
    - Formally launched in **1988** (Odisha passed the first resolution).
        
    - Local village institutions undertake protection activities in return for non-timber forest produce and a share in harvested timber.
