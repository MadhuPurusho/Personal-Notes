## 1. Water Scarcity and the Need for Conservation

- **Water Availability:** About **97.5%** of the world's water is saline ocean water, leaving only **2.5%** as freshwater. Nearly 70% of freshwater is stored in glaciers and ice sheets.
    
- **Causes of Water Scarcity:**
    
    - **Quantitative Causes:** Over-exploitation, excessive use, and unequal access among different social groups.
        
    - **Population Growth:** Growing population increases demand for food and domestic use, putting pressure on groundwater through tubewells for intensive agriculture.
        
    - **Industrialization & Urbanization:** Industrial energy needs (hydroelectric power) and expanding urban centers with deep groundwater pumps lower the water table.
        
    - **Qualitative Causes:** Pollution from domestic and industrial wastes, chemicals, pesticides, and fertilizers renders water unsafe for human use.
        

## 2. Multi-Purpose River Projects and Integrated Water Resources Management

Traditionally, dams were built to store rainwater for agriculture. Modern **Multi-Purpose River Projects** serve multiple goals simultaneously.

### Key Advantages of Multi-Purpose Projects

- Hydroelectric power generation
    
- Flood control and soil conservation
    
- Irrigation for agriculture
    
- Domestic and industrial supply
    
- Fish breeding, navigation, and inland waterways
    
- Recreation and tourism

|**Bhakra-Nangal**|Sutlej-Beas|Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan (Hydel power & irrigation)|

### Disadvantages and Criticisms of Large Dams

Jawaharlal Nehru proudly referred to dams as the **"Temples of modern India"** because they integrated agriculture with industrial growth. However, they face significant criticism:

- **Ecological Impact:** Damming rivers alters natural flow, causing excessive sedimentation at the bottom, making riverbeds rockier and destroying aquatic habitats.
    
- **Displacement of Communities:** Construction submerges vast forest areas and displaces local and tribal populations without proper rehabilitation.
    
- **Environmental Degradation:** Reservoir creation submerges vegetation, leading to decomposition and greenhouse gas emission.
    
- **Induced Hazards:** Large reservoirs can trigger earthquakes, cause waterborne diseases, and spread pest infestations.
    
- **Inter-State Disputes:** Clashes over sharing water releases and costs (e.g., Krishna-Godavari dispute, Cauvery water dispute).
    
- **Social Movements:** Triggered resistance movements like **Narmada Bachao Andolan** and **Tehri Dam Andolan**.
    

## 3. Rainwater Harvesting

Rainwater harvesting is a traditional, eco-friendly, and cost-effective alternative to large dams for meeting water needs.

```
                   Traditional Rainwater Harvesting Methods
                                      |
         +----------------------------+----------------------------+
         |                            |                            |
   Hilly Regions              Arid Plains (Rajasthan)      Flood Plains (Bengal)
   • Guls / Kuls              • Johads / Khadins           • Inundation Channels
   • Diversion channels       • Roof top rainwater (Tanka) • Irrigating fields
```

### Traditional Techniques Across Regions

1. **Guls / Kuls (Western Himalayas):** Diversion channels built in mountain zones to direct natural stream water to agricultural fields.
    
2. **Inundation Channels (Bengal):** Channels dug in flood plains to irrigate fields using floodwaters.
    
3. **Khadins and Johads (Rajasthan):** Agricultural fields converted into rain-fed storage structures that allow water to stand and moisten the soil (e.g., Khadins in Jaisalmer, Johads in other regions).
    
4. **Rooftop Rainwater Harvesting & Tankas:**
    

- Common in arid and semi-arid regions like Bikaner, Phalodi, and Barmer.
    
- Rainwater from roofs travels down pipes into underground storage tanks called **Tankas**.
    
- The first flush of rain is discarded to clean the roof; subsequent rainwater (**Palar Pani**) is stored for drinking during summer.
    

### Modern Practice & Success Stories

- **Tamil Nadu:** First state in India to make rooftop rainwater harvesting compulsory for all houses across the state by law.
    
- **Gendathur Village (Karnataka):** A remote village in Mysore where nearly 200 households installed rooftop rainwater harvesting systems, earning a reputation for water self-sufficiency.
    
- **Bamboo Drip Irrigation System (Meghalaya):** A 200-year-old system using bamboo pipes to transport stream water directly to the roots of plants over long distances.
