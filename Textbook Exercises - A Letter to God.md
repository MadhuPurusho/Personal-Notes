## 1. Oral Comprehension Check

### Page 5

#### Q1. What did Lencho hope for?
**Answer:** Lencho hoped for a good downpour or at least a light shower for his fields of ripe corn, which were crucial for a healthy harvest.

#### Q2. Why did Lencho say the raindrops were like 'new coins'?
**Answer:** A good rain meant a rich harvest, which would bring him financial prosperity. Therefore, he compared the big raindrops to **ten-cent pieces** and the small ones to **five-cent pieces**.

#### Q3. How did the rain change? What happened to Lencho's fields?
**Answer:** A strong wind began to blow, turning the rain into a severe **hailstorm**. Hailstones fell continuously for an hour, destroying the entire corn crop and leaving the fields completely white as if covered with salt.

#### Q4. What were Lencho's feelings when the hail stopped?
**Answer:** Lencho’s soul was filled with deep sorrow and despair. Seeing his destroyed field, he felt that they would go hungry that year as all their hard work had gone in vain.

#### Q1. Who or what did Lencho have faith in? What did he do?
**Answer:** Lencho had deep, unshakeable faith in **God**. He believed that God sees everything, even what is deep in one's conscience. Consequently, he wrote a letter to God asking for **100 pesos** to sow his field again and support his family until the next crop.

#### Q2. Who read the letter?
**Answer:** The **postmaster** read the letter after a postman brought it to him laughing.

#### Q3. What did the postmaster do then?
**Answer:** To preserve Lencho's innocent faith in God, the postmaster decided to reply to the letter. He collected money from his employees, contributed a portion of his salary, and received donations from friends, gathering **70 pesos** in total. He mailed it back to Lencho signed simply as **"God"**.

**Answer:** When Lencho counted the money, he found only **70 pesos** instead of the **100 pesos** he had demanded. Believing that God could neither make a mistake nor deny him his request, he assumed the post office employees had stolen the missing 30 pesos.

#### Q1. Who does Lencho have complete faith in? Which sentences in the story tell you this?
**Answer:** Lencho has complete faith in God.  
**Sentences in the story:**
* *"But in the hearts of all who lived in that solitary house in the middle of the valley, there was a single hope: help from God."*

#### Q2. Why does the postmaster send money to Lencho? Why does he sign the letter 'God'?
**Answer:** The postmaster was deeply moved by Lencho’s unwavering belief. He did not want to shake the writer's faith in God. By signing the letter **"God"**, he ensured Lencho would believe the money came directly from the Almighty.

#### Q3. Did Lencho try to find out who had sent the money to him? Why or why not?
**Answer:** No, Lencho did not try to find out who sent the money. He had absolute confidence that God would answer his prayer, so he never questioned the origin of the letter.

#### Q4. Who does Lencho think has taken the rest of the money? What is the irony in the situation?
**Answer:** Lencho believes the **post office employees** stole the remaining 30 pesos.  
**Irony:** The situation is deeply ironic because the very post office workers who selflessly collected money out of goodwill were called a **"bunch of crooks"** by Lencho.

### Concept 1: Relative Clauses

Relative Clauses are clauses starting with relative pronouns such as **who, whom, whose, which, that** used to join two sentences or give additional information about a noun.

* **Who:** Used for people (subject position).
* **Whom:** Used for people (object position).
* **Whose:** Used to show possession.
* **Which / That:** Used for things, animals, and non-living objects.

#### Textual Exercise:
*Join the given sentences using who, whom, whose, or which:*

1. **Question:** I often go to Mumbai. Mumbai is the commercial capital of India. (*which*)  
   **Answer:** I often go to Mumbai, which is the commercial capital of India.

2. **Question:** My mother is going to host a TV show on cooking. She cooks very well. (*who*)  
   **Answer:** My mother, who cooks very well, is going to host a TV show on cooking.

3. **Question:** These sportspersons are going to meet the President. Their performance has been excellent. (*whose*)  
   **Answer:** These sportspersons, whose performance has been excellent, are going to meet the President.

4. **Question:** Lencho prayed to God. His eyes see into our minds. (*whose*)  
   **Answer:** Lencho prayed to God, whose eyes see into our minds.

5. **Question:** This man cheated me. I trusted him. (*whom*)  
   **Answer:** This man, whom I trusted, cheated me.

### Concept 2: Emphatic Negatives

In literary writing, negative structures (such as *not a*, *never*, *nothing less than*) are frequently used to express positive or strong ideas with greater emphasis.

#### Textual Exercise:

1. **Idea:** The trees lost all their leaves.  
   **Textual Sentence:** *"Not a leaf remained on the trees."*

2. **Idea:** The letter was addressed to God himself.  
   **Textual Sentence:** *"It was nothing less than a letter to God."*

3. **Idea:** The postman saw this address for the first time in his career.  
   **Textual Sentence:** *"Never in his career as a postman had he seen that address."*

### Concept 3: Metaphors in the Text

A **Metaphor** is a figure of speech that directly compares one object or action to another to highlight a shared characteristic, without using explicit comparative words like "like" or "as".
