## 1. What are Life Processes?

- **Definition**: Basic processes required to maintain life in living organisms (e.g., nutrition, respiration, transport, excretion).
    
- **Energy Requirement**: Maintenance processes prevent damage and breakdown, requiring energy provided by food via chemical reactions.
    
- **Molecular Movement**: Invisible molecular movements are essential for life to repair and maintain organized cellular structures.
    
- **Oxidative-Reductive Reactions**: Common chemical means used by bodies to break down carbon-based molecules to yield energy.
    

## 2. Nutrition

- **Definition**: The process of taking in food and utilizing it for growth, repair, and energy.
    
- **Modes of Nutrition**:
    
    1. **Autotrophic**: Organisms make their own food using simple inorganic substances.
        
    2. **Heterotrophic**: Organisms depend on other organisms for food.
        

### 2.1 Autotrophic Nutrition

- **Process**: [[Photosynthesis]] — autotrophs take in $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, converted into carbohydrates in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll.
    
- **Storage**: Carbohydrates not used immediately are stored as **starch** (in plants) or **glycogen** (in animals).
    
- **Chemical Equation**:  
    
    $$6\text{CO}_2 + 12\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow[\text{Chlorophyll}]{\text{Sunlight}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
- **Key Events in Photosynthesis**:
    
    1. Absorption of light energy by chlorophyll.
        
    2. Conversion of light energy to chemical energy and splitting of water molecules ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) into hydrogen and oxygen.
        
    3. Reduction of carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) to carbohydrates.
        
- **Stomata**: Tiny pores on leaves responsible for gaseous exchange and transpiration. Guard cells control their opening and closing by swelling (water enters) or shrinking (water exits).
    

### 2.2 Heterotrophic Nutrition

- Organisms obtain energy by consuming organic compounds created by autotrophs.
    
- **Types**:
    
    - **Holozoic**: Ingestion and internal digestion of solid food (e.g., Humans, Amoeba).
        
    - **Saprophytic**: Breakdown of food material outside the body, then absorption (e.g., Fungi like bread moulds, yeast, mushrooms).
        
    - **Parasitic**: Deriving nutrition from plants or animals without killing them (e.g., Cuscuta/Amarbel, ticks, lice, leeches, tapeworms).
        

### 2.3 How do Organisms obtain their Nutrition?

- **Unicellular Organisms**:
    
    - **Amoeba**: Uses temporary finger-like extensions called **pseudopodia** to fuse over food particles forming a food vacuole. Complex substances are broken down into simpler ones inside and diffuse into the cytoplasm.
        
    - **Paramecium**: Has a definite shape; food is taken in at a specific spot moved by the movement of **cilia** covering the cell surface.
        

### 2.4 Nutrition in Human Beings

The **Human Alimentary Canal** is a continuous tube extending from mouth to anus.

```
Mouth ➔ Oesophagus ➔ Stomach ➔ Small Intestine ➔ Large Intestine ➔ Anus
```

- **Mouth (Buccal Cavity)**:
    
    - Teeth grind food.
        
    - Salivary glands secrete **Saliva** containing **Salivary Amylase** (breaks down starch $\rightarrow$ simple sugar/maltose).
        
- **Oesophagus (Food Pipe)**:
    
    - Pushes food forward via rhythmic muscular contractions called **Peristaltic movements**.
        
- **Stomach**:
    
    - Gastric glands secrete:
        
        - **Hydrochloric Acid (**$\text{HCl}$**)**: Creates an acidic medium ($\text{pH} \approx 1.5-2.0$) and activates pepsin.
            
        - **Pepsin**: Protein-digesting enzyme.
            
        - **Mucus**: Protects stomach lining from acid corrosion.
            
- **Small Intestine**:
    
    - Site of complete digestion of carbohydrates, proteins, and fats.
        
    - Receives secretions from:
        
        - **Liver**: Secretes **Bile juice** (emulsifies large fat globules into smaller globules; makes medium alkaline).
            
        - **Pancreas**: Secretes **Pancreatic juice** containing **Trypsin** (digests proteins) and **Lipase** (digests emulsified fats).
            
    - Intestinal wall secretes enzymes converting:
        
        - Proteins $\rightarrow$ **Amino acids**
            
        - Complex Carbohydrates $\rightarrow$ **Glucose**
            
        - Fats $\rightarrow$ **Fatty acids + Glycerol**
            
    - **Absorption**: **Villi** (finger-like projections) increase surface area for maximum absorption into blood vessels.
        
- **Large Intestine**:
    
    - Absorbs excess water from unabsorbed food material.
        
    - Waste is removed from the body via the **Anus** (regulated by the anal sphincter).
        

## 3. Respiration

- **Definition**: Process of acquiring oxygen from outside the body and using it to break down glucose for cellular energy release stored as **ATP** (Adenosine Triphosphate).
    

### 3.1 Breakdown of Glucose by Various Pathways

Glucose ($6\text{-carbon molecule}$) is converted in the cytoplasm into **Pyruvate** ($3\text{-carbon molecule}$) + Energy.

```
                        ┌─► Absence of O₂ (Yeast) ──────────► Ethanol + CO₂ + Energy (2 ATP)
                        │
Glucose ──► Pyruvate ───┼─► Lack of O₂ (Muscle Cells) ──────► Lactic Acid + Energy
 (6-C)       (3-C)      │
                        └─► Presence of O₂ (Mitochondria) ──► CO₂ + H₂O + Energy (38 ATP)
```

1. **Anaerobic Respiration (Fermentation)**:
    
    - Occurs in absence of $\text{O}_2$ (e.g., Yeast).
        
    - End products: Ethanol + $\text{CO}_2$ + minimal energy.
        
2. **Anaerobic in Muscle Cells**:
    
    - Occurs during vigorous exercise when $\text{O}_2$ is lacking.
        
    - End products: **Lactic acid** + energy. Accumulation of lactic acid causes **muscle cramps**.
        
3. **Aerobic Respiration**:
    
    - Occurs in presence of $\text{O}_2$ in **Mitochondria**.
        
    - End products: $\text{CO}_2$ + $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ + high energy yield.
        

### 3.2 Human Respiratory System

- **Path of Air**:  
    
    $$\text{Nostrils} \rightarrow \text{Pharynx} \rightarrow \text{Larynx} \rightarrow \text{Trachea} \rightarrow \text{Bronchi} \rightarrow \text{Bronchioles} \rightarrow \text{Alveoli}$$
- **Trachea**: Rings of cartilage prevent the airway from collapsing.
    
- **Alveoli**: Balloon-like structures providing a large surface area for gaseous exchange. Surrounded by an extensive network of blood vessels.
    
- **Mechanism of Breathing**:
    
    - **Inhalation**: Ribs lift, diaphragm flattens, chest cavity expands $\rightarrow$ air sucked into lungs.
        
    - **Exhalation**: Ribs move down, diaphragm relaxes/curves upward $\rightarrow$ air pushed out.
        
- **Respiratory Pigment**: **Hemoglobin** (present in RBCs) has a high affinity for oxygen and transports it throughout the human body.
    

### 3.3 Respiration in Plants

- Gaseous exchange occurs through **stomata** in leaves, **lenticels** in stems, and general root surfaces via **diffusion**.
    
- **Direction of diffusion** depends on environmental conditions and plant requirements:
    
    - **At Night**: No photosynthesis $\rightarrow$ $\text{CO}_2$ elimination is major event.
        
    - **At Day**: $\text{CO}_2$ generated during respiration is used for photosynthesis $\rightarrow$ $\text{O}_2$ release is major event.
        

## 4. Transportation

### 4.1 Transportation in Human Beings

#### A. Circulatory System Components:

1. **Heart**: Muscular pumping organ with 4 chambers (prevents mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood):
    
    - **Right Atrium & Right Ventricle**: Receive and pump deoxygenated blood.
        
    - **Left Atrium & Left Ventricle**: Receive and pump oxygenated blood.
        
2. **Blood Vessels**:
    
    - **Arteries**: Carry oxygenated blood away from heart (except pulmonary artery). Thick, elastic walls; no valves.
        
    - **Veins**: Carry deoxygenated blood toward heart (except pulmonary vein). Thin walls; contain **valves** to prevent backflow.
        
    - **Capillaries**: Single-cell thick vessels where exchange of material between blood and tissues occurs.
        
3. **Blood**: Liquid connective tissue consisting of **Plasma**, **RBCs**, **WBCs**, and **Platelets** (responsible for blood clotting at injury sites).
    

#### B. Double Circulation

Blood passes through the heart **twice** during one complete cardiac cycle.

1. **Pulmonary Circulation**: Deoxygenated blood from heart $\rightarrow$ Lungs $\rightarrow$ Oxygenated blood to heart.
    
2. **Systemic Circulation**: Oxygenated blood from heart $\rightarrow$ Body tissues $\rightarrow$ Deoxygenated blood to heart.
    

#### C. Lymphatic System

- **Lymph**: Interstitial fluid collected into lymphatic capillaries.
    
- **Function**: Carries digested/absorbed fats from intestine and drains excess fluid from extracellular space back into blood.
    

### 4.2 Transportation in Plants

Plants do not have a circulatory system; transport relies on vascular tissues:

1. **Xylem**:
    
    - Transports **water and minerals** unidirectionally from roots to aerial parts.
        
    - Consists of vessels and tracheids.
        
    - **Driving forces**:
        
        - **Root Pressure**: Pushes water up in small plants or short heights.
            
        - **Transpiration Pull**: Suction pressure created by loss of water vapor through stomata (**Transpiration**); primary force during the day.
            
2. **Phloem**:
    
    - Transports **soluble products of photosynthesis** (sucrose, amino acids) bidirectionally.
        
    - Process called **Translocation**.
        
    - Requires energy in the form of **ATP** to create osmotic pressure differences that move material into tissues.
        

## 5. Excretion

- **Definition**: Biological process involved in the removal of harmful metabolic toxic wastes from the body.
    

### 5.1 Excretion in Human Beings

- **Human Excretory System Components**:
    
    - A pair of **Kidneys**
        
    - A pair of **Ureters**
        
    - A **Urinary Bladder**
        
    - A **Urethra**
        

#### Nephron — Functional Unit of Kidney

- Each kidney contains microscopic filtering units called **Nephrons**.
    
- **Structure**:
    
    - **Bowman's Capsule**: Cup-shaped structure containing a network of capillaries called the **Glomerulus**.
        
    - **Tubular Part**: Henle's loop, proximal/distal convoluted tubules.
        
- **Urine Formation Steps**:
    
    1. **Glomerular Filtration**: Nitrogenous wastes (urea, uric acid), glucose, amino acids, salts, and water are filtered from blood into Bowman's capsule.
        
    2. **Selective Reabsorption**: Useful substances like glucose, amino acids, salts, and major amounts of water are reabsorbed into surrounding blood capillaries as fluid moves through the tubule.
        
    3. **Tubular Secretion**: Excess ions/wastes are secreted into the tubule forming urine.
        
- **Storage**: Urine collected in collecting ducts enters ureters, flows to the urinary bladder, and is excreted via the urethra under nervous control.
    

### 5.2 Excretion in Plants

Plants use different strategies for excretion:

- **Gaseous Wastes**: $\text{O}_2$ and $\text{CO}_2$ released through stomata.
    
- **Transpiration**: Excess water removed as vapor.
    
- **Cellular Vacuoles**: Waste products stored in cellular vacuoles or leaves that eventually fall off.
    
- **Resins and Gums**: Waste products stored in old xylem tissues.
    
- **Soil Excretion**: Plants also excrete some waste substances into the soil around their roots.
