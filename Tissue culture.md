# Tissue Culture (Plant Tissue Culture)

**Tissue culture** (or _in vitro_ culture) is a technique used to grow new plants from small plant tissues or cells in an artificial, sterile nutrient medium under controlled environmental conditions.

It relies on **cellular totipotency**—the inherent capacity of a single living plant cell to divide, differentiate, and regenerate into a whole organism.

## Process & Steps

1. **Explants:** Small tissue samples (e.g., shoot tips, leaves, or buds) are taken from the parent plant and surface-sterilized.
    
2. **Inoculation on Nutrient Medium:** The explant is placed on a sterile culture medium containing:
    
    - Inorganic nutrients (macronutrients and micronutrients)
        
    - A carbon source (sucrose)
        
    - Growth regulators/hormones (Auxins and Cytokinins)
        
    - A gelling agent (Agar)
        
3. **Callus Formation:** The cells divide rapidly to form an unorganized, undifferentiated mass of cells called a **callus**.
    
4. **Organogenesis (Plantlet Formation):** The callus is transferred to media with varied plant growth hormone ratios to induce shoot and root formation, developing into tiny **plantlets**.
    
5. **Hardening & Acclimatization:** The plantlets are gradually transferred to soil inside green-houses before being planted in fields.
    

## Advantages of Tissue Culture

- **Micropropagation:** Allows rapid multiplication of large numbers of plants in a short period and in a small space.
    
- **Disease-Free Stock:** Plants can be grown free from viruses by using healthy apical meristems as explants.
    
- **Year-Round Production:** Enables growth independent of seasonal changes or weather conditions.
    
- **Seedless & Rare Plants:** Helps propagate species that have lost the capacity to produce viable seeds (e.g., banana, seedless grapes) or are endangered.
    
- **Genetically Uniform Clones:** Produces offspring (**somaclones**) that are identical to the parent plant.
