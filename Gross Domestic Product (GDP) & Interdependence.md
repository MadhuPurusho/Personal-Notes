## Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

### Meaning

Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is the value of all final goods and services produced within a country during a particular year.

It helps us understand the size and performance of an economy.

### Calculation of GDP

GDP is calculated by adding the value of goods and services produced by the three sectors:

- Primary sector
- Secondary sector
- Tertiary sector

### Example

- Agriculture produces wheat.
- A factory uses wheat to produce packaged food.
- Transport and shops help distribute and sell the product.

The value of final goods and services is counted in GDP.

## Importance of GDP

- Measures the total production of an economy.
- Helps compare economic performance over time.
- Shows the contribution of different sectors to the economy.
- Helps the government plan economic policies.

# Interdependence of Economic Sectors

The three sectors of the economy are interdependent. They depend on one another for the production and distribution of goods and services.

## Example

Consider the production of a cotton shirt:

1. Primary sector → Farmers grow cotton.
2. Secondary sector → Factories convert cotton into cloth and shirts.
3. Tertiary sector → Transport, banking, communication and shops help produce, distribute and sell the shirt.

Therefore, the sectors cannot work completely independently.

## Another Example

Agriculture depends on:

- Factories for tractors, fertilisers and machines.
- Banks for loans.
- Transport for carrying crops.
- Shops and markets for selling produce.

Industries depend on:

- Agriculture for raw materials.
- Transport for moving goods.
- Banks for financial services.
- Communication for business activities.

The service sector depends on both agriculture and industries because these sectors create demand for services such as banking, transport, insurance and trade.

## Key Point

The primary, secondary and tertiary sectors are closely connected. Growth or decline in one sector can affect the other sectors.

# Quick Revision

GDP → Value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a year.

GDP includes → Primary + Secondary + Tertiary sectors.

Interdependence → The three sectors depend on one another for production, distribution and consumption.
