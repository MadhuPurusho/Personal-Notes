## 1. Types of Farming

Agriculture is an age-old economic activity in India. Depending upon the characteristics of physical environment, technological know-how, and socio-cultural practices, farming systems vary:

### A. Primitive Subsistence Farming

- **Nature:** Practiced on small patches of land using primitive tools (hoe, dao, digging sticks) and family/community labor.
    
- **Dependence:** Relies entirely on monsoon, natural fertility of the soil, and favorable environmental conditions.
    
- **Slash and Burn Agriculture:** Farmers clear a patch of land, produce food crops, and when soil fertility decreases, they shift to a new patch.
    
    - Allows nature to replenish soil fertility naturally; land productivity is low as no modern fertilizers are used.
        
    - **Regional Names:**
        
        - _Jhumming_ (North-Eastern states like Assam, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland)
            
        - _Pamlou_ (Manipur), _Dipa_ (Bastar, Chhattisgarh, and Andaman & Nicobar Islands)
            
        - _Milpa_ (Mexico), _Roca_ (Brazil), _Ladang_ (Indonesia).
            

### B. Intensive Subsistence Farming

- **Nature:** Practiced in areas of high population pressure on land.
    
- **Features:** It is labor-intensive farming where high doses of biochemical inputs and irrigation are used to obtain higher yield.
    
- **Land Holding Problem:** The 'right of inheritance' leads to division of land among successive generations, making landholding size small and uneconomic.
    

### C. Commercial Farming

- **Nature:** Main characteristic is the use of higher doses of modern inputs (High Yielding Variety - HYV seeds, chemical fertilizers, insecticides, pesticides) to achieve higher productivity.
    
- **Plantation Farming:** A type of commercial farming where a single crop is grown on a large area.
    
    - Combines agriculture and industry using capital-intensive inputs and migrant labor.
        
    - Requires a well-developed network of transport and communication connecting plantation areas to markets and processing industries.
        
    - **Examples:** Tea (Assam and North Bengal), Coffee (Karnataka), Rubber, Sugarcane, Banana.
        

## 2. Cropping Pattern in India

India has three distinct cropping seasons:

|**Cropping Season**|**Sowing Period**|**Harvesting Period**|**Key Crops Grown**|**Major Regions**|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**Rabi**|Winter (Oct – Dec)|Summer (April – June)|Wheat, barley, peas, gram, mustard.|Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Uttarakhand, UP.|
|**Kharif**|Onset of monsoon (June – July)|Autumn (Sept – Oct)|Paddy (rice), maize, jowar, bajra, tur (arhar), moong, urad, cotton, jute, groundnut, soybean.|Assam, West Bengal, coastal Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Maharashtra.|
|**Zaid**|Short summer season between Rabi and Kharif (March – June)|Summer months|Watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, vegetables, fodder crops.|Across river basins and irrigated tracts.|

## 3. Major Crops Grown in India

A variety of food and non-food crops are grown across the country depending on soil, climate, and cultivation practices.

### A. Rice (Paddy)

- **Importance:** Staple food crop of a majority of the people in India. India is the **second-largest producer** in the world after China.
    
- **Crop Type:** Kharif crop.
    
- **Climatic Requirements:**
    
    - **Temperature:** High temperature (above **25°C**).
        
    - **Rainfall:** High humidity with annual rainfall above **100 cm**. In areas of less rainfall, it grows with the help of irrigation.
        
- **Major Regions:** Plains of north and north-eastern India, coastal areas, and deltaic regions. Punjab, Haryana, and Western UP produce quality rice using canal irrigation and tubewells.
    

### B. Wheat

- **Importance:** Second most important cereal crop; main food crop in north and north-western parts of India.
    
- **Crop Type:** Rabi crop.
    
- **Climatic Requirements:**
    
    - **Temperature:** Requires a cool growing season and bright sunshine at the time of ripening.
        
    - **Rainfall:** 50 to 75 cm of annual rainfall evenly distributed over the growing season.
        
- **Major Zones:** Ganga-Satluj plains in the north-west and black soil region of the Deccan.
    
- **Major States:** Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, Rajasthan.
    

### C. Millets (Coarse Grains)

Millets have very high nutritional value. The three major millets grown in India are:

1. **Jowar:** Third most important food crop with respect to area and production. Rain-fed crop mostly grown in moist areas.
    
    - _Major States:_ Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh.
        
2. **Bajra:** Grows well on sandy soils and shallow black soil.
    
    - _Major States:_ Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Haryana.
        
3. **Ragi:** Rich in iron, calcium, other micro-nutrients, and roughage. Dry region crop grown on red, black, sandy, loamy, and shallow black soils.
    
    - _Major States:_ Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Jharkhand.
        

### D. Maize

- **Importance:** Used both as food and fodder.
    
- **Crop Type:** Primarily a Kharif crop (though grown in Rabi season in Bihar).
    
- **Climatic Requirements:**
    
    - **Temperature:** Between **21°C to 27°C**.
        
    - **Soil:** Grows well in old alluvial soil (_Bangar_).
        
    - Use of modern inputs like HYV seeds, fertilizers, and irrigation has contributed to increasing production.
        
- **Major States:** Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh.
    

### E. Pulses

- **Importance:** India is the **largest producer as well as consumer** of pulses in the world. Major source of protein in a vegetarian diet.
    
- **Key Pulses:** Tur (arhar), urad, moong, masur, peas, gram.
    
- **Features:**
    
    - Require less moisture and survive even in dry conditions.
        
    - **Leguminous crops:** Except arhar, all pulses help in restoring soil fertility by fixing nitrogen from the air. Hence, usually grown in rotation with other crops.
        
- **Major States:** Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka.
